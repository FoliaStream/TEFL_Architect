**Introduction to Speaking**

Speaking, along with writing, comprises one of the output components, or **production** components, of language. While we chose to package the production skills of language into this chapter, language courses generally combine speaking with listening, as these two skills are closely connected. Speaking **practice** commonly follows listening practice, which you learned about in chapter 8.

Conversation classes are one of the most popular and common language curricula for learners of all ages. In modern **ESL/FH**, pedagogy, the teaching of speaking follows an eclectic **approach**, combining methods and materials in a manner that encourages oral output that is reflective of the lesson’s language goals.

Teaching speaking can be highly rewarding, as watching students improve their oral **language skills** increases opportunities for you to communicate with them and learn more about them as individuals. It’s certainly an exciting and important part of **TEFL**, so let’s dive in!
