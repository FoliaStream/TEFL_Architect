**Sample Speaking Activities**

Below is a non-exhaustive list of speaking activities and a brief description of each. Note that some of these activities have been addressed in previous chapters in the coursebook.

- **Songs**

Songs can be used in classrooms with students of any age. For young learners, song activities coupled with gestures or images is common practice and can be an effective way to engage students with the lesson topic and aim. When selecting for beginning levels, use songs with simple repetitive lyrics. See chapter 5 for electronic resources related to using songs in the ESL/EFL classroom.


---

- **Role Plays**

Students assume the role of a character in a situational dialogue. These activities can vary from rote recitation to open-ended conversations, and can be used in basic or complex contexts, so this technique is good for students at all levels of acquisition.

---

- **Information Gap**

This is generally done in pairs; one student has information that the other doesn't, and vice versa. For example, a common type of gap activity is for two students to have different versions of the same map (e.g., missing buildings, street names, etc.). They must negotiate and exchange information to find out the missing details.

---

- **Prepared Speeches/Presentations**

Here, the teacher provides the topic and structure for what the students will produce, while providing some flexibility in terms of content. The topic and length should reflect the speaker’s level of language acquisition. To ensure audience attention, assign responsibilities to the listener (e.g., taking notes, offering peer feedback).

---

- **Problem Solving**

Students are given some information and must find and propose a solution to a problem. Good for high-intermediate and advanced students as a production activity. Give a time limit for discussion and have them report to the class to ensure they stay on task.

---

- **Open-Ended Discussion**

If open-ended discussion is chosen as a class activity, students need to have a clear idea of what they are discussing, why, and what is expected of them. Make sure to provide guidance and preparation beforehand, and follow-up afterward.

---

- **Interviews and Questionnaires**

These can be written by the teacher or by students with guidance from the teacher. Interviews and questionnaires are good for production outside of the classroom.

---

- **Audio and Video Recording**

Students record themselves individually or in pairs/groups. Topics and tasks can be structured, or more open-ended. These have the benefit of being done without teacher monitoring, which can be beneficial for introverted students.

---

- **Video Pen Pals**

You may use Skype or another form of video conferencing. Video correspondence is effective for authentic communication production with native speakers of English but may take some time and effort to set up. You will learn more on this topic in chapter 10 – Visual Aids and Technology.

---

- **Games**

There are many games that can be used to practice speaking and can be adapted for all levels, including BINGO; Charades; Twenty Questions; What's my line? Two Truths and a Lie; Find Someone Who…; and Jeopardy! An Internet search will reveal a great variety of resources and pre-made templates specifically designed for the ESL/EFL classroom.
