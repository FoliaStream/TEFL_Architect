**Basics of Second Language Writing**

Second language writing **production** can take many forms - personal tasks, **ESP** (academic and business) writing, creative writing, etc. The kind of writing you teach will be dependent on your students' needs and goals. However, regardless of what you cover in class, always use the following guidelines as a foundation (Nation, 2009).

- Do a **needs analysis** at the onset of instruction. A good writing sample not only serves as an artifact of each students' writing at the beginning of a semester or school year to measure progress or compare actions vs. in-class writing, but it will assist with planning your curriculum, lessons, and activities.
- Have students bring their background experience and knowledge to their writing tasks, and have them draw on their interests. Having some input on topic choice works well in this regard.
- Have them do lots of writing and different types in order to expose them to a wide variety of genres, organizational patterns, rhetorical styles, and language.
- There should be a purpose for their writing. The content of the **task** should be accessible to the writer, and the final product should communicate a clear message to the reader.
- Assigned tasks should have clear instructions and expectations for the final product. Provide students initially with a model writing sample that reflects what you expect them to produce. They should also know the evaluation criteria beforehand.
- Student language ability should be adequate and sufficient to meet the requirements of the task.

Also, consider the types of writing tasks that are appropriate for young learners. Teachers typically do not begin incorporating writing activities in young-learner classrooms until students are able to read and write in their native language. Once students are learning to write in their native language, you may begin to incorporate very simple writing activities (e.g., copying single letters or words, completing fill-in-the-blank worksheets, or completing picture crosswords) into the **EFL** classroom.
