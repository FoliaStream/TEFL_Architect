**Designing Lessons and Activities**

At this point in the textbook, you should start to recognize similarities and distinctions between practicing the four **language skills**. Relate the following four steps to what you already know about executing reading and listening skill practices in your classes.

*Step 1. Create Context (Warm-up):* Set the scene for the activity and activate student background knowledge. This is critical because **ESL/EFL** students benefit from **schema activation** prior to being required to **practice** language skills.

*Step 2. Present the Target Language:* Present the key concept that is at the heart of the lesson. This can be done by using authentic speech samples while focusing student attention on the target language.

*Step 3. Practice the Target Language:* Have students practice the language through controlled pair or group activities that are closely monitored by the teacher. These can include short and structured conversations, role plays, close exercises, or choral repetition.

*Step 4. Produce the Target Language:* Have students use the new language to communicate effectively, in a context that mirrors real life as much as possible. Monitoring is minimal at this stage, and students should be given the opportunity to use the language in a less structured format, independently, and outside the classroom if possible. This is the stage where the communicative aspects of language come into play.
