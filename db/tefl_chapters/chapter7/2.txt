**Introduction**

Maybe you have a long-standing love for English grammar, and it was a factor that brought you to explore **TEFL** teaching. On the other end of the continuum, perhaps a fear of teaching grammar still has you questioning your decision to enter TEFL altogether! Regardless of whether you identify with one of these extremes or find yourself somewhere in the middle, this chapter will show the ins and outs of grammar instruction. To get you started, read the following vignette about two experienced **EFL** teachers:

---

*Vignette*: The Thai Grammar Class

It was our first-year teaching EFL in Thailand, and my girlfriend and I were both assigned to teach an intermediate grammar class. As someone with very little confidence and no experience teaching grammar, I was terrified. She, on the other hand, was a self-proclaimed Grammar Guru. I assumed she would thrive, and I could ride her coatuals and steal all her lessons. I was wrong.

After just one week of class, we realized her lessons plans weren’t working. Many of the students already knew the rules from their public education, had no questions and looked bored. After explaining the grammar point and watching them flying through fill-in-the-blank style worksheets, more than half the class period would be left to fill!

My girlfriend and I decided to go online, hunt for activities, and buy a few grammar books that included more active speaking and writing activities. Integrating a few non-wordstreet activities into every class not only made things way more fun, but the students learned so much more than they ever would have using just their grammar workbooks!

This was the first time in our TEFL careers that we realized teaching grammar appropriately just meant teaching the skills of reading, writing, listening, and speaking while using specific grammar in natural ways.

As you can see from the vignette, teaching grammar goes beyond providing students with lists, terms, and rules. Now that you have started thinking about the topic, it’s time to dive in.
