**Final Considerations for the Humanistic Methods and Approaches**

'While the original enthusiasm for these methods and approaches has faded since they were first introduced, they all contain some important components. CLL gave us the concept of modifying the
curriculum to meet our students' needs and interests. The Silent Way gave us the idea that students can build a passive linguistic knowledge before they actively use the language, and it also gave rise to
the idea of connecting new language with something that learners already know, like colors. The Silent Way also encouraged students to construct their own knowledge. Suggestopedia emphasized the
importance of the environment, the teacher's effect on the learning environment, and the role that music can play in the learning process. TPR introduced the idea of combining kinesthetic and visual
stimuli to push learners to learn language in an active way. TPRS built on this to teach language in context while using story-based discourse and brief grammar explanations.

All of these approaches put the student at the center of the learning equation for the first time and attempted to put learning in their hands, whereas with early western methods students were seen
