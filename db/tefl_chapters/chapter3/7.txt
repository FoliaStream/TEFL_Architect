**Grammar Translation Method**

The *Grammar Translation Method* is based on the belief that language learning is a type of mental training and that the learner's mind is stimulated through direct translation between languages. As many of us who have learned another language know, we often try to translate between our native language and our new language. This method is based on the assumption that the more students do this, the better their language abilities will be.

The pitfalls of this method can be seen in the video. Almost all instruction occurs in the native language of students, and very little English is used. Students might be able to read vast amounts of language through this method, but they never arrive at conversational speaking or listening abilities needed for everyday communication. This method also excludes very young learners who are not yet able to read or write in their native language.

This model of language learning was used as the principle way of learning languages for thousands of years and was refined in Germany in the 18th century into a method (Richards & Rodgers, 2001). However, it slowly fell out of favor due to people's inability to speak the language being learned. There are many teachers who still use this method today, at least in part. In fact, if you ask your students to study vocabulary using a vocabulary journal, you are asking them to use this method. You can have your students study vocabulary in this fashion outside of class to limit the amount of time students spend translating in the classroom.
