**TPR and TPRS Takeaways**

Many teachers like to use TPR to introduce or review basic vocabulary and grammar. The method allows teachers to stay away from students' native languages and only use the target language. This is a
real benefit for beginner level classes because the teacher does not have to worry about knowing the students' primary languages in order to teach them. TPR is often fun and engages students on
multiple levels through the use of kinesthetic and visual activities, and many students appreciate not being forced to produce language until they are ready. TPRS takes TPR further by giving students the
language in context and having more of a focus on connected sentences, which is ideal for many students, especially young learners. It also allows for brief grammar explanations, appealing to some

institutional requirements or student learning preferences. By using connected discourse within (hopefully) interesting stories, TPRS also helps students to feel more comfortable with discussion in the
classroom, once they feel that they are ready for it.
