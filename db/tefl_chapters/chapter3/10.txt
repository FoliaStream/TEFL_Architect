**Takeaways from Early Language Teaching Methods**

The methods mentioned will rarely, if ever, be condoned in isolation by any school or teacher. However, you will find a lot of holdovers from them in current language books and in many teachers' language classrooms. **Table 2** details each **method** or **approach** listed in this section with some suggested ideas that you can take away from them.

*Table 2.* Early methods

| Method    | Pros    | Cons    | Takeaways    |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar translation   | Learners have strong grammar.    | Learners never use the language in a natural context.    | Since students will translate many materials by instinct, see what materials, such as vocabulary, you can have them translate and study in this fashion at home to improve their language base independently.    |
|    | Relies on the natural process of translation that occurs in many learners' minds | It does not take advantage of having learners learn from their mistakes in producing original statements.    |    |
| Direct    | Learners are able to acquire a lot of language quickly by connecting concepts to target language. | Lexical clarity is difficult. For instance, you might teach the word "cup," but then students never learn the word "glass." They may think the only thing used for liquids is a cup.    | This is a great way to present new language to students.    |
| Audiolingual    | Learners improve pronunciation and learn language in context. | Abstract concepts that are intangible are difficult to teach.    | It can be used as a springboard into other methods and language structures.    |
|    |    | Learners memorize large portions of information but are not taught how to problem solve or function in real situations in which answers and language are different than classroom language models. | The dialogues from ALM are great samples to use as examples for students.    |
|    |    |    | Teachers can structure audio materials around ALM exercises so that students can work on listening skills at home |

While there are clear disadvantages to these methods, they each have some strong points. Grammar Translation can be used by learners at home to **practice** vocabulary and grammar. Elements of the **Direct Method** are cornerstones in many language classrooms. The **Audiolingual Method** works well to give examples of real-world conversations that show the vocabulary in context and as a way to have learners practice listening outside of the classroom through guided practice. The benefit of these methods is that they have been widely used for a long time, so you can more readily find examples that adhere to their principles and material on how to implement them in complement to other methods.
