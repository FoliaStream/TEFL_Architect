**Communicative Approach**

The *Communicative Approach* is a common term used in language programs throughout the world today. It is also one of the most misused terms in teaching because there is an alternative term called *Communicative Language Teaching* (CLT). The Communicative Approach is a general philosophy and not a specific **method** or **technique** that you use in the classroom, and its definition will vary depending on your source. According to Richards and Renandya (2003), the Communicative Approach is based on a belief that all teaching and classroom activities should be conducted in English, the target language.

A good example is a simple grammar exercise. You can have students work on it in pairs or individually, but the preparation and instructions for the activity are all done in English, the review of the activity is done in English, and students talk to each other in English while they work together to complete the activity. Students then take this knowledge and use it afterward to complete a speaking, listening, or writing activity, again using only English to communicate.

*Misunderstandings with the Communicative Approach*

Some people think that if they speak to their students only in English, then they are using the Communicative Approach (Lee & Van Patten, 2003). However, this is not the case. While the teacher is in charge of teaching and the students are in charge of learning, communication has to happen on all fronts. The students, as well as the teacher, need to be speaking English in the classroom in all interactions that occur. Lee and Van Patten (2003) state that just speaking in the foreign language to students who do not understand what is being said results in students being "talked at." Our goal should be talking with our students in the target language and having students talk with their peers in the language that we are teaching in order to help them improve their language abilities. New teachers may be surprised to learn that teaching involves a dialogue rather than a monologue. It is true that there will be times that students might not understand everything that we say; however, our goal in a communicative classroom is to have students understand most of the language being used and to have them use the language in as natural a way as possible, learning through trial and error.

*Other considerations of the Communicative Approach*

The Communicative Approach to language teaching is one of the key philosophies that language teachers subscribe to today. In the past, most language teaching focused on memorizing language and learning it in an unnatural way, with the belief that students would be able to recall this information later and use it outside the classroom in real-life situations. Language classes that have too much focus on memorization, however, do not give students the opportunity to practice real-world speaking or writing tasks. Basically, if your students are not using the language to learn it, to ask and answer questions in order to navigate meaning, then they are not really learning the skills they need to function in English. Applying the Communicative Approach allows us to teach students how to use the language as they learn it so that they are better prepared for communication outside the classroom. This breaks from past approaches that gave students language they did not really know how to use.
