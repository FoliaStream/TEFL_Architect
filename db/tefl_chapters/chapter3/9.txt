**Audiolingual Method (ALM)**

The *Audiolingual Method* (ALM) is characterized by memorization of dialogues, as well as use of language labs. Originally called the "Army method," ALM began in the 1940s as a result of World War II (Griffiths & Parr, 2001, p. 247). This method requires language to be memorized for use in structured conversations in the classroom (Larsen-Freeman, 2000). When ALM was first introduced, it was thought that if people could remember their part of the conversation, then they would be able to function in the language by knowing the cues that would drive them towards speaking in the language being taught. All instruction was in the target language and taught in a way that was seen as natural.

ALM fell out of favor in teaching in the late 1960s/early 1970s as a reaction to the idea of language learning being connected to meaning (Omaggio-Hadley, 2001, p. 115). The method relied on learners guessing and forming meaning on their own. However, there were no guarantees that learners actually understood meaning because there was no focus on meaning. This is the main reason why ALM is not part of the **Communicative Approach** that is in favor today: students exposed to ALM do not learn the meaning of the language they are supposed to recite. Many learners also commented that, after traveling to the country where the target language was spoken, native speakers did not recite parts of conversation exactly as it was taught in the classroom. The natural differences in conversation left students unable to respond.

While many of the traditional methods were replaced by contemporary methods, it is still possible to come across these older methods around the world. Today, anytime you pick up a book that contains a conversation that students can complete together at the beginning, you are seeing some of the leftovers of the ALM era. You will also find books that contain recorded conversations that students listen to in order to learn key phrases. There is nothing wrong with incorporating these two elements into any language classroom; however, you never want this to be your sole method of language teaching.
