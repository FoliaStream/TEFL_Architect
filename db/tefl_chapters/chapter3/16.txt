**Community Language Learning (CLL) Approach**

Community Language Learning (CLL) breaks with previous approaches because the environment in which learning occurs precedes all other aspects of the learning process. According to Nagaraj (2009), CLL focuses on student-to-student and student-to-teacher relationships to establish a healthy language learning environment (p. 177). It is believed that once trust has been built between all parties in the classroom, there are no blocks to learning. You might remember a good classroom experience you've had in the past by the relationship you had with your teacher and classmates.

Nagaraj (2009) lists the five stages of CLL as "birth, self, separate existence, adolescence, and independence" (p. 178). See the following Table 3.

*Table 3*. CLL stages, definitions, and connections

| Stage    | Definition    | Illustration    |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1- Birth    | Learners know none of the language they are learning and rely on their teacher for all language that they need.    | Think back to your first day of a foreign language class. This is probably how you felt on that day and during the first weeks of class.    |
| Stage 2- Self-learners    | Learners are beginning to develop understanding of the language but still depend on the teacher.    | Think about the first couple of foreign language courses you took; you probably felt like this.    |
| Stage 3- Separate Existence    | Learners begin to use the language or their own.    | This would be like reaching a second-year university language course or a third-year high school language course, where you feel comfortable with the basics, but you realize that you still need to work on more advanced languages skills.    |
| Stage 4 - Adolescence    | Learners function independently. However, when they realize they are missing language, they go to their teacher for help.    | This would be like finishing a two-year foreign language course or a four-year class in high school and trying to use the language in the real world. You are able to function with the basics, but you rely on locals or others more proficient in the language to help you learn the content you are missing.    |
| Stage 5 - Independence    | Learners function on their own without the teacher and begin to occupy the teacher role for less advanced students.    | Many times, this happens naturally in classrooms because many people who catch on more quickly help those who still have not grasped previously-learned concepts.    |

Adapted from Nagaraj (2009, p. 178)

Community Language Learning is a student-centered approach; the classroom is a positive and fully functioning learning community. Students pick the content that is learned, and the teacher is more of a guide through the language learning process. This approach works well, but problems may arise if there is no set curriculum or set way for schools to tell students what they will learn. This approach is still used in many parts of the world today. Think about what you can take away from this approach to individualize it for your own classroom.
