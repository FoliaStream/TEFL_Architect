Breakdown of humanistic methods and approaches

**Community Language Learning (CLL)**
*Pros*: CLL is student-centered and focuses on maintaining an optimal learning environment. 
*Cons*: The content is completely student derived, which makes it impossible to have a standard curriculum. It is difficult to track students' progress between different courses.
*Takeaways*: All classes end up with a curriculum that has to be modified to cater to students' needs and interests. A student-centered classroom empowers learners and allows them to take ownership and responsibility for their learning.


**Silent Way**
*Pros:* The environment is tailored to be the best for students to learn in the most comfortable way. Teacher influences students in a positive way.
*Cons:* Many of the changes needed to teach with this method are not possible in most classroom environments.
*Takeaways:* Environment can influence students' overall language learning experience, and music can be used as a positive tool to passively influence that environment.


**Suggestopedia**
*Pros:* The environment is tailored to be the best for students to learn in the most comfortable way. Teacher influences students in a positive way.
*Cons:* Many of the changes needed to teach with this method are not possible in most classroom environments.
*Takeaways:* Environment can influence students' overall language learning experience, and music can be used as a positive tool to passively influence that environment.


**Total Physical Response (TPR)**
*Pros:* Students learn a lot of language by connecting verbal language to kinesthetic and visual activities. Students can have a lot of language input and experience before they have to actively use the language.
*Cons:* Students do not learn language outside of commands. Students might be copying others or making false connections with the language. Also, students do not learn language for natural conversations.
*Takeaways:* TPR allows students who are at lower levels to learn language quickly. Students learn vocabulary faster because they can integrate more senses. TPR can be used to present new language or **practice** it.

**Total Physical Response Storytelling (TPRS)**
*Pros*: TPRS allows for in-context language learning. Students can have conversations, and grammar can be taught.
*Cons*: Stories might not be relevant to all students. Students might not grasp all concepts taught in the lesson.
*Takeaways*: TPRS allows for teachers to teach grammar and allow for conversations with a reading. It is also a way to teach language-specific gestures.
