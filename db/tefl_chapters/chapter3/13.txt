**Natural Approach**

The Natural Approach, which was developed in response to the early teaching methods described in the previous sections, is one that attempts to teach a new language in a way that mirrors the way people naturally learn their first language. As Omaggio-Hadley (2001) relates, an approach to mirroring natural language use should have three main points: 1) listening activities as initial activities, 2) all activities focused on communication, and 3) little attention is given to error correction. Listening is key to children learning their first language, and the same is true for learners who are acquiring a new language. Teachers who used the Natural Approach would implement a lot of listening activities that included simplified language at the beginning student level, and communication would connect to that listening throughout the class session to help learners build their language abilities. It was also believed that communication could occur without a strong explicit knowledge of grammar. Therefore, the building of vocabulary was of the utmost importance, and grammar rules were largely ignored and replaced by repetition of examples. Error correction was not seen as important because children do not learn from correction while acquiring their first language; children learn through the use of the language, and the kinks work themselves out as they go.

The Natural Approach eventually evolved into what is easily one of the most popular second language teaching approaches today: The Communicative Approach.
