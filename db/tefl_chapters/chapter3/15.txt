**Humanistic Approaches and Methods to Language Teaching**

In the 1960s and 1970s, many new approaches and methods emerged that were based on how people were treated in the language classroom and how they felt about language learning (Omaggio-
Hadley, 2001, p. 123). Commonly called "designer methods," these were a reaction to past language approaches and methods that seemed to lack a welcoming atmosphere for learners. These
approaches and methods also emerged because it was becoming even more apparent that past approaches and methods by themselves were not enough to ensure optimal language learning.
Educators and researchers alike were on the lookout for the one "perfect" approach or method, and each of these approaches and methods was heralded as the one that would replace all of those
previous. Note the differences and similarities of these approaches and methods with others you have learned about and consider what parts of them you would like to use in your classroom.
