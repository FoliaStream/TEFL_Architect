>**INTRODUCTION**

There are various teaching approaches / methods / techniques:
- **Approach**: belief of philosophy about language learning
    
Students exchange information about each other and the teacher personalizes topics

- **Methods**: plan or general outline of how to put the approach into action 

Students perform tasks where they must relate classroom topics to their own cultural and / or method 

- **Technique**: specific task or activity that aligns with the pre-determined approach and method 

After presenting food vocabulary / grammar students interview each other about common food traditions. Then students write summary

>**TRADITIONAL WESTERN METHODS TO TEACH LANGUAGES**

EU and USA methods are popular - focused on grammar

*3 methods*:
- Grammar translation
- Direct
- Audiolingual

When popular, streamlined to work with situation --> over time opinions changed but still partially found 

---

>**GRAMMAR TRANSLATION METHOD**

Based on belief that language learning is a type of mental training

Learners mind stimulated through direct translation between languages

The more you translate between native and new language the better

*Pitfall*:
- difficult converstaional speaking / listening activities
- exclude young Learners
- fell out favor due to people's inability to speak 

Can still use for grammar studies

---

>**DIRECT METHOD**

Instructors teaching language by directly associating words to pictures / objects in language taught 

A bit language learned in short time 

Effective with young learners - move beyond ideas / events

Disadvantage - no way to teach actions / abstract ideas

*Total Physical Response (TPR)* - extension of this method

---

>**AUDIOLINGUAL METHOD (ALM)**

Memorization of dialogues and use of language labs 

Originally army method - started in 40s as result of WWII

Language memorized for use in structured conversations in class - people remember conversations, able to functino in the language by knowing cues drivign them 

ALM fell out of favor in 60s-70s - language learning being connected to meaning

ALM - relied learners guessing / forming meaning on their own but no focus on meaning --> ALM not part of *Communicative Approach*

>**ACCURACY VS FLUENCY**

*Accuracy* - sentences have correct grammar / vocab -> traditional teachers: Audiolingual / Grammar translation + exercises (difficult communication with native speakers)

*Fluency* - expressing ideas easily and with appropriate speed (even if with errors)

**Need to balance** : 
- fluency exercises - role plays 
- accuracy exercises - grammar awareness

Teacher - focus on aim of class to decide focus fluent/accurate

>**NATURAL APPROACH**

Attempt to teach new language in a way that mirrors the way people naturally learn their first language 

**Omaggio-Hadley** - method with 3 main points:
- initial activities - listening 
- all activities focus on communication 
- little attention on correction 

Listening is key to children learning - a lot of activities at beginning student level 

Communication connect to the listening to build activities - can occur without expicit grammar knowledge 

Building vocabulary - ignored / replaced by repetition of examples 

Children learn through the use of the language - kinks work themselves out as they go 

Natural appraoch evolved into the **Communicative Approach**

---

>**COMMUNICATIVE APPROACH**

Common tearm in language programs - also very misused term - confused for the *Communicative Language Teaching* (CTL)

Communicative Approach -> RIchards, Rendaya: all teaching activities conducted in the target language

This is an "Approach" so it is a general phylosophy - not specific method or technique

*Misunderstanding with the Communicative Appraoch*:
- Students and teacher neet to be speaking in class in all interactions 
- goal: talking to students in target language and having students talking to each other in englih to make them improve 
- they will not understand everything - but the goal is to have them understand the most and make them use it in the natural way 

*Other implications*:
- Past - language teaching focused on memorizing language -> unnatural 
- Too much focus on memorization - no opportunity to practice 
- Students not using language to learn it - not really learning it 
- Communicative approach - learn how to use the language as they learn it

>**COMMUNITY LANGUAGE LEARNING (CLL) APPROACH**

Breaks with previous approaches 

Environment in which learning occurs precedes all other aspects of learning process 

Once trust built - no blocks to learning 

*Nagaraj* - 5 stages of the CCL:
- **Birth**: learners know none of the language - 100% depend on teacher
- **Self-learners**: learners begin to develop understanding - still depend on teacher 
- **Separate-existence**: begin to use language alone - comfortable with basics work on advanced 
- **Adolescence**: independent learning - go to teacher for help 
- **Independent**: no teacher needed - act as teacher for others 

CCL - student-centered approach 

Class is a positive and fully functioning learning community 

Students pick content learned 
Teacher is a guide in the process

>**SILENT WAY**

Founder - Caleb Gattegno 

Knowledge cannot be transmitted - awareness of knowledge can be taught 

Teachers let students figure out answers - discover their own 

First language acquisition - learn absorbing language around; learners of additional language can do the same 

Richards, Rodgers: 
- rods and colo coded pronunciation charts (Fidel charts)
- physical foci for students 
- create memorable images 

Use colors to establish sound system - make own rules based on color - help connecting abstract concepts 

Students figure our sound system based on what they piece together from input in class 

People figure out rules of language

>**SUGGESTOPEDIA**

Humanistic method most far from Western mainstream language teaching

Idea of lowering students inhibitions -> important for language learners of all ages 

Humanistics foreign teaching method that uses a combination of techniques - physical relaxation / visualization exercises / affirmations and relaxing music based on strengthening self-esteem 

Teacher - be sure to create environment that is conductive to learning 

*Optimal environment*:
- room painted with colors - invite / entice learners 
- paintworks to adorn walls to optimize comfort 
- music used to relax students 

2 types of music used: 
- baroc music - passive concert for the presentation of new info 
- classical music - active concert 

Teachers - leaders make environment positive; use this method as student-centered to accomodate different students 

Teacher influences environment for students 


>**TOTAL PHYSICAL RESPONSE (TPR)**

Connect words/action/pictures

**TPR**:
- gained most exposure and lasted the most 
- direct evolution of Direct Method - next step to address deficiency 

Not new - introduced by Palmer in Tokyo, called "Teaching through actions" (1925)

James Ahser -> 60s/70s better pioneering; best way to make language stick with person is to make as many connections as possible (*trace theory*)

TPR - avoid using native language 

Students receives much input before starting class 

Simon says - TPR approach 

*Widodo (2005)* Asher TPR has 3 pillars based on principle learning second language is mirror of learning the first:
1. Before speaking, children listen - blueprint creation 
2. Children acquire listening comprehension - need to respond physically to spoken language (parental commands)
3. Once listening comprehension established - speech evolves naturally 

Students do not speak much in early phase of TPR - as it is not required, but it is a great way to teach vocabulary / commands

Critics: 
- lack of authentic dialogue seen in everyday scenario
- cannot reach abstract concepts via TPR 


---

>**TOTAL PHYSICAL RESPONSE STORYTELLING (TPRS)**

Teaching Proficiency through Storytelling and Reading 

Same premise as TPR - lot of input given before studs required to speak/write

TPR pitfall: people learn action language out of context - real speakers always in context 

Story told throughout class period: 
- use specific gestures during lesson 
- sentences of story repeated many times 

first time(s) - teacher and students use gestures together to *represent selected language in story*

Then, teacher stops moving - students use same movements in story connection to target language 

Slightly change story to ensure not simply memorized 

End of class - students recite whole story with gestures 

**TPRS**:
- students more in context learning 
- give students more comprehensible input before speak/write
- grammar on limited scale (not possible in TPR) 

---

>**TAKEAWAYS TPR - TPRS**

- introduce / review vocab / grammar
- stay away from native language 
- use only target language
- real benefit for beginner level 
- fun and engaging 

TPRS takes TPR to the next level:
- give context language
- brief grammar explanation 
- more comfortable with discussions

>**HUMANISTIC METHODS APPROACHES**

**CLL - Community Language Learning**: 
- *Pros*: student-centered, focused on maintaining optimal environment 
- *Cons*: content student derived so impossible standard curriculum and difficult tracking 
- *Takeaways*: all curriculum modified to cater studs need / interests; allow students take responsibility for own learning 

**SILENT WAY**:
- *Pros*: connect sound and colors, build own rules on observation
- *Cons*: teacher must set up class in specific way, materials must be ready 
- *Takeaways*: different colors for different language structures 

**SUGGESTOPEDIA**
- *Pros*: environment tailored to be the best / most comfortable; teacher influences in positive way 
- *Cons*: many changes needed are not possible in classroom environment
- *Takeaways*: environment influence overall experience, musci positive tool 

**TPR - Total Physical Response**:
- *Pros*: learn language by connecting verbal languge to kinesthetic; a lot of input before class provided 
- *Cons*: studs do not learn outside commands; may make false connections by copying others, no natural context 
- *Takeaways*: studs low level learn quickly, integrate more senses 

**TPRS - Total Physical Response Storytelling**:
- *Pros*: in-contect language learning; students can have conversations; grammar can be taught 
- *Cons*: stories might not be relevant to all; may not grasp all concepts 
- *Takeaways*: allow teachers to teach grammar and allow for conversations; teach language-specific gestures

>**CONTENT-BASED INSTRUCTION (CBI)**

Method can manifest itself in many ways 

Emphasis learning about something rather than language 

No focus on grammar - focus on content / context or pronunciation

Theme-based approach to teaching - topic / theme chosen 

CBI - more common at higher level 

---

>**TASK-BASED LEARNING (TBL)**

Require teachers think about real-world task - give it to students to complete

Structure:
- pre-task
- task preparation
- post-task 

TBL - open to interpretation and implementation open to possible forms 

Drawback - strong focus on fluency over accuracy

---

>**COOPERATIVE LEARNING**

Learning through cooperation - group work!!!

Some students comfortable/other don't 

Overall better to work in small groups 

Encourage interaction among students 

Students different level work together 

---

>**JIGSAW METHOD**

Students work together in same way of each piece of puzzle 

Each students does one part of activity - pass over to others 

Reduce stress of having to complete tasks in front of entire class 

Allow students to teach each other in comfort zone 

Teacher as monitoring / organizer 

Takes a lot of planning 

---

>**PROJECT-BASED LEARNING (PBL)**

Using projects (involve research)

Student-centered approach that engages students in exploring important questions through investigation / collaborations 

Incorporate students interests 

Students independent from teacher

Great preparation needed from teacher - instructuions on how to proceed 

Students take ownership of project 

---

>**MULTISENSORY APPROACH**

Attempt to engage senses in teaching 

Learn subject using 2-or-more senses

Combination of senses = higher probability appealing different learning preferences 

Combinations: 
- pictures-words 
- picturess-sounds
- tastes-words 
- ...

Activities:
- flashcards
- realia
- auditory 

Improve student comprehension of language 

Speed up time to learn 

Avoid use of native language
