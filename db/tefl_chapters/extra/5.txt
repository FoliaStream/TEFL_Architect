>**DIVERSITY IN LANGUAGE COURSES**

Language programs: vary in shapes / sizes due to diverse attitudes towards it - different countries / schools 

*Best practices*: ask school administration some program - related questions before first day - **during interview!!**

**Questions**: 
- What are the learner goals of program? 
    - test preparation
    - general English 
    - full immersion 
    - listen-speak focus 
    - business English 
- What are learning methods/philosophies used by school? 
- What materials (if any) provided by school? books/plans/syllabus
- How many hours x week? Intensive language? Homework expected? 
- How many instructions and in what capacity?
- What are students demographics and how affect program? 
- Handbook about students behaviour? 


*Basic components of language course program*
- Learning objectives 
- Lesson plans
- Materials 
- Course policies 
- Form of assessment

>**BASIC STEPS IN COURSE DESIGN**

Required thoughtful big-picture planning 

**HOW** to teach -> method/approach and planning individual lessons and activities 

**WHAT** to teach -> grammar / lexis / practice and how to fit them 

*Overview of steps* - maybe asked before time of start / maybe asked to complete part before and part during course 
- **Step 1.** Identify situation and students, account for needs and priorities of school program 
- **Step 2.** Deliver needs analysis of students 
- **Step 3.** Translate data from needs analysis into course goals and learning objectives 
- **Step 4.** Determine course content/sequnce/structure and evaluate/choose materials (textbooks)
- **Step 5.** Design course syllabus / include schedule of units / assignments and assessments
- **Step 6.** Construct lesson plans
- **Step 7.** Teach! Revise steps 4-6 as needed
- **Step 8.** Assessment

Completing steps depends on school, but overall structure is to be used 

---

>**NEEDS ANALYSIS**

Key step student-centered classroom - needs analysis 

Collect data on individuals - data used to inform the creation of tailored objectives 

Needs analysis - several methods but the easiest is **survey** - ask open ended and multiple choice questions 

*Survey focus questions*: 
- educational background 
- culture
- language abilities
- motivations 
- interests
- aspirations 

Multiple choice questions:
- more effective 
- easy data to analyse 
- convenient and easy to complete
- limited in scope - only selected amount of data

Consider students don't have clear perceptions of their own abilities - not aware of language skills needed / inaccurate view of current language abilities 

Need to use different forms of assessment - possibly *diagnostic assessment* - identify learners' strengths and weaknesses 

**Diagnostic assessment** - tools used to determine gaps in students abilities / weaknesses in particular skills: 
- formative assessment - not formal / summative evaluation 
- used to determine progress at edn of course 
- establish scope/sequence of course with framework for feedback / improve

>**CREATING COURSE GOALS**

Formulate some overarching goals for course 

Course goals ≠ Learning objectives 

- Learning objectives - SWBAT framework at end of lesson
- Course goals - learger aims of extended period of time, broad course content and tasks 

**Examples of course goals**: 
- gain confidence in reading high-beginner/advanced texts 
- write several short texts / paragraphs / summaries / emails 
- improve listening comprehension
- practice speaking and improve pronunciation by participating in conversation / giving presentation 
- comprehend / communicate beginner-to-intermediate strctures 
- build vocab
- develop general awareness of language culture 

Goals are broad and generic scope

>**UNITS OF STUDY**

Breakdown course into units - themes / topics / grammar structures 

Look for materials / textbooks for that unit 

Otherwise - can choose one book that fits needs and use it to structure course units 

If book provided by school - evaluate it 

Break course into smaller blocks - often grammatical structures 

Typical EFL intermediate-level textbook: 
- each unit one grammar structure 
- followed by vocabulary 
- communicative acts 
- skill practice 

Alternative organize by theme / topic - beginner-level textbook: 
- units organized by theme/topics 
- grammar and skills practice come after 
- most young learners books

>**SYLLABUS**

Useful tool of communication when you meet students 

Syllabus - helps to establish broad timeframes for course goals / expactations 

First days - critical time -> ideal to make syllabus understood: 
- set time / place / foundation for rest of term 
- let students know what to expect from them / what they expect from you 
- clear syllabus - help achieve goals / create positive effective environment 

Common items: 
- bulleted list of easy to understand goals 
- broad schedule of units of study / topics / assessment in course 
- information on grades and scores (A-F, 1-10)
- your work (not personal) contact info 
- policies regarding tardiness
- policies regarding late work 
- additional policies such as phone use / target language use / academic honesty 

Info on syllabus decided on program / institutional environment 

My responsibility to ensure classroom expectations fairly / consistently applied 

Review content and syllabus - ask questions / quizzes about it
