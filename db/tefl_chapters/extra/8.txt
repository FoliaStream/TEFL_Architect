---

>**LISTENING PROCESSING**

*Key terms*:

- **Bottom-up Processing**:
    - Students focus on specific parts of language - keywords/structures
    - Language-focused rather than meaning-focused 
    - Practice: students listen for specific details-grammar/vocab/pronunciation
    - Beneficial for beginning-level students 
    - **Intensive listening**

- **Top-down Processing**:
    - Students focus on overall meaning of language - start from what they know topic/situation
    - Meaning-focused rather than language-focused 
    - Practice: pre-listening tasks trigger background knowledge 
    - **Extensive listening**

Students must be given activities addressing both - both processes in place = interactive processing 

Goals - listening:
- listener tune into speaker to understand how perform action 
- simply for employment / pleasure (music)

Anyway use both processes 

---

>**COMMON STUDENT DIFFICULTIES**

Listening capabilities vary - class/studs - complex and diverse 

*Difficulties*:
- **Students may try to understand (or translate) every word**:
    - Trying to translate every word - they stop listening when face new words, fail to understand overall meaning 
    - Use top-down listening to facilitate 
- **Students may not recognize sounds**:
    - Particularly for beginner-students 
    - Practice sound distinction on minimal pairs - pairs of words with only phonetical difference 
    - Ask students to circle word they hear - vibration of vocal chords - voicing 
- **Students may not recognize reduced forms**:
    - Hearing reduced forms - common parts of language including contractions / elision / assimilation / reduction 
    - Bottom-up practice on pronunciation 
- **Students may not understand speech with unfamiliar stress/rythm/interaction**:
    - Suprasegmental features - important of how language sound 
    - Unfamiliar stress patterns - interfere recognition word - syllabes longer/louder/higher pitch or change pronunciation/meaning 
    - Sentence level - stress on content words, function words are de-stressed 
    - Combination of stressed /unstressed words -> called rythm 
    - English - stressed-time language: practice stress at sentence level 
    - Intonation - they way pitch goes up/down as we speak 
    - practice may be beneficial to understand meaning 
- **Other difficulties - Different English / Backgroun noise**

> **TYPES OF LISTENING - INTENSIVE VS EXTENSIVE**

**Intensive Listening**

Listening with specific purpose in mind - details / info 

Bottom-up processing - focus on specific features

*Activities*:
- Prediction questions to focus on specific info 
- T/F or Y/N questions on specific details 
- Wh-questions for specific info 
- Fill-in-blank question/exercise 
- Sentence completion 
- Error correction
- Listen sound / pronunciation
- Ticking / numbering / ordering 
- Fill graphs info 


**Extensive listening**

Listening for overll meaning / purpose / attitude 

Top-down - focus on overall context 

*Activities*:
- Questions on main ideas / setting 
- T/F or Wh-questions on meaning 
- T/F or Wh-questions on inference 
- Questions to connect background knowledge 
- Questions to give opinion on listening 
- Summary 
- Synthesis multiple listening

>**READING PROCESSING**


**Bottom-up Processing**
Decode word / sentence structure line by line 

First decipher parts to understand text as whole 

Practice: 
- students read for specific details of text
- identify affixes 
- grammar/vocab/content 


**Top-down Processing**

From general idea of meaning to more specific one 

Read section - make hypothesis on meaning - read more to confirm / reject hypothesis 

Practice:
- students predicct topic by reading title, substitle, pictures 
- annotate comments 
- identify main idea 

*Concept driven* 

Combination of these processes - metacognition 

Metacognitive view of reading: 
- reader thinks conciously about read strategies
- metacognitive skills driven intensive reading lessons

Engage students interest and activate schema / background knowledge

Activities: 
- brainstorming activities to ask what know about topic 
- ask what know about text structure
- skim reading main ideas by read headings / subheadings / details 
- pre-teach key words 


**During-reading**

Gain practice in build reading skills 

Activities: 
- ask main idea/detail/inference questions 
- ask meaning questions from context 
- "think aloud" in group to create predictions of meaning 
- groups create questions for each other 
- students annotate text 
- use / create graphical organizers 

**Post-reading**

gain greater exposure / practice with target language from reading in production task 

- reading story - students write letter to characters / explain actions to others 
- reading job advertisments - students write application letter 
- reading piece from news - students write paragraph agree / disagree / opinion
- ask summary if level ok 
- pair work - question on reading
