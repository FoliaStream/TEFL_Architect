>**MORPHEMES**

Morphemes - smalles unit of language that carries meaning 

**Free** Morphemes: set of letters that can stand on its own and have meaning (cold, move, happy, etc.)

**Bound** Morphemes: must be attached to free morphemes (-er, un-, etc.)

Specific type of bound morphemes - **inflectional morpheme**: added to word without changing its part of speech 

- plural **-s** and **-es** added nouns 
- possessive **'s** added to nouns 
- verbs ending **-ing, -s, -ed, -en** to show tense 
- suffixes **-ex, -est** added to adjectives 

---

>**WORDS ROOTS, PREFIXES, AND SUFFIXES**

Understanding of various word parts can be used to improve learning of words through relating unknown word forms and meaning to known word parts - important to know how words constructed 

- Word root: free morphene as main part of word 
- Prefix: bound morphese placed at beginning and alters meaning 
- Suffix: bound morphene attached to the end and alters meaning of word - sometimes can also simply change nouns to plural / indicative tense of verb

>**COLLOCATION AND PHRASAL VERBS**

When teaching new words - teach also collocations to improve proper usage 

Collocations - the way words go together 

*Examples*: 
- (verb) commit: murder/a crime/treason 
    - never say commit a favour
- (noun) appointment: make/keep/cancel
    - meeting synonym - cannot make/break, can keep/cancel 

**Phrasal Verbs**: verbs that contain main verb and particle 
- putting verb and particle together alter meaning to create new one 
- run out / pick up / picked out / get over / get up / etc..

>**LINGUISTIC UNDERSTANDING - 5 WAYS**

Teaching language - you are an applied linguistic 

Linguistics - 5 ways of understanding language: 

1. **Phonology**: 
    - recognize how language organizes phonemes (sounds) in order to covey meaning
    - also includes pronunciation and how form sounds physically with mouth/throat 

2. **Morphology**: 
    - word formation: understanding word structure, root words, new words, word changes, morphemes

3. **Syntax**: 
    - sentence formation - understanding how words function together to create units like phrases / clauses / sentences 
    - includes word order, sentence formation, question formation, parts of speech 

4. **Semantics**: 
    - meaning - knowing the meaning of a language words' common synonyms, definitions, metaphorical meanings 
    - learning vocab - form of semantic understanding 

5. **Pragmatics**: 
    - context - knowing what language is appropriate to use according to context - question of formality 
    - often learnt implicitly by observing peer 
    -  know when appropriate to use particular word
