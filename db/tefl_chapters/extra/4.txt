>**LANGUAGE LEVELS**

One of the first questions - consider language level of students 

Usually divide students in: **beginner** / **intermediate** / **advanced** (-> potential sub categories such as sub/mid/high for each level)

**National Reporting System - NRS** - 6 levels:
- Beginning literacy 
- Low-beginner
- High-beginner
- Low-Intermediate
- High-Intermediate
- Advanced 

Levels defined by skills and ability - **SWBAT** format: Students Will Be Able To ...

---

>**LISTENING INDICATORS**

Learners may demonstrate listening comprehension non-verbally, and *will be able to...*

- recognize letters of words when spelled / dictated 
- recognize / respond to greetings simple questions and polite expressions in routine and familiar settings 
- recognize / respond to simple questions and statements in familiar contexts 
- follow one-step directions 
- identify simple expressions indicating lack of understanding 
- recognize / respond to requests for repetition
- recognize / respond to personal info 

**ACTFL** - student samples of reading / writing / listening / speaking 

Proficiency at different levels guidelines 

Remember students more proficiency than others, and some students may differ in their own by school - range of proficiency may vary largery: learn range in proficiencies of studs vie **NEEDS ANALSYSIS**

---

>**SPEAKING INDICATORS**

*Learners will be able to...*

Spell some familiar words
Use numbers appropriately to provide personal information
Produce and respond to common greetings, introductions, and polite expressions
Produce simple statements in routine and familiar situations
Use the telephone to make an emergency call
Respond to simple questions in familiar situations demonstrating some control of grammar

--- 

>**READING INDICATORS**

*Learners will be able to...*

Apply sound/symbol relationships to decode familiar words (e.g., high-frequency words for life skills)
Read cursive and printed materials
Interpret words and symbols on everyday signs
Scan for specific information on familiar documents (e.g., utility bill, pay stub)
Follow some simple written instructions that include pictures or diagrams (e.g., food preparation, simplified prescriptions, care labels)
Follow simple geographical directions on a simple map
Read some common sight words
Read simple sentences on a familiar topic

---

>**WRITING INDICATORS**

*Learners will be able to...*

Write basic personal information (name, address, telephone number, employer, birth date, ID number, etc.)
Write familiar simple words and short phrases from dictation
Complete short, simplified forms (e.g., check, job application, registration form)
Address an envelope
Write lists (e.g., shopping) and personal schedules
Write some simple phrases and simple sentences related to familiar situations
Use some simple basic punctuation (e.g., capitalization, periods, commas, question marks)

---

>**PRONUNCIATION INDICATORS**

*Learners will begin to recognize and practice the following...*

Chunks (words that often go together) and linking the words together [It's a _, = Itza]
Letter-sound associations (phtonemes)
Recognize/produce intonation for yes/no questions
Recognize/produce intonation for Wh-questions

---

>**GRAMMAR INDICATORS**

*Learners will be able to demonstrate a beginning understanding of the following structures and patterns...*

Simple Wh-questions and Yes/No questions and responses (affirm./neg.)
Affirmative and negative statements with "be, do, have" and other high-frequency verbs
Simple present continuous tense and simple past with high-frequency verbs such as "be, do, have"
Modal auxiliary verbs (e.g., can / have to + verb)
Singular/bural (e.g., count/non-count)
Simple Conjunctions (and, or, but)
Simple prepositions of time and place (e.g., in, on, at, next to, on the right)
Simple frequency adverbs (e.g., always, sometimes, never)
Possessive Adjectives (e.g., my, your, her, our) and Subject Pronouns

>**LESSON PLAN OBJECTIVES**

Most important component of lesson plan - list of *objectives*:

learning objectives - result of lesson:
- use action verbs 
- written from perspective of what learner will be able to do at the end of lesson (not what taught)

**SWBAT Format**:

*By the end of the lesson, students will be able to...*
- ask / answer pertinent questions / ngotiate date / time 
- demonstrate comprehension of 10 relevant doctor appointment vocab 
- produce questions using when/what time/howlong
- produce appropriate / polite forms of greeting / requests

Include objectives in every lesson - structure / sequence / plan out goal of classes 

Elements to be included in plan: 
- **Timing**: how long each activity take
- **Assumptions**: what studs must be able to do before lesson to complete successfully lesson 
- **Interaction**: who is speaking to whom in each part of lesson - student talking time & teacher talking time 
- **Materials**:  what books / activities / websites to use 
- **Homework**: what must students do as follow-up before lesson

>**LESSON PLAN STAGES**

Many lesson plan formats available online 

Many variants - no accepted format 

All include same elements of language input - language practice - student output 

Common format -> **Presentation - Practice - Production (PPP)**

7 stages: 
- Warm-up 
- Introduction 
- Presentation 
- Practice 
- Production 
- Review 
- Homework 

---

>**WARM-UP STAGE**

Greeting students and starting lesson 

*"Entry point"* - designed to help studs ease into second language in low pressure way 

- Recycle key components of previous lessons 
- Activate background knowledge 
- Schema for current one 

Common activity warm-up:
- present 1/2 open questions about upcoming topic 
- give 2-3 minutes to discuss answers in groups 
- add pictures /realia to boost communication 

*Example*
- Travel:
    - bring souvenirs of own experience
    - show busy scene at airport / bus station / hotel and ask to describe in group 
- Order food in restaurant (beginner)
    - what restaurants do you like? 
    - what jobs are in restaurant? 
- Identify theft (intermediate)
    - do you keep info from internet?
    - what do you if lost ID?
- Make good impression (advanced)
    - do you think eye contact is important? 
    - what behaviours employer look for? 

---

>**INTRODUCTION STAGE**

Short sub-stage designed to bring theme of lesson and content of presentation in focus 

Warm-up - connected to lesson theme 

Introduction 
- introduce theme
- find what studs already know
- get students thinking 

Images / realia / questions / general discussion 

Pre-teach some vocab to comprehend - beware include diverse representations 

Can also show students activities planned 

*Fine to change/adjust according students*

---

>**PRESENTATION STAGE**

Learners exposed to meaningful examples of languge concepts of lesson 

2 ways: 
- **inductively**: start with use of language before 
- **deductively**: start with form / meaning 

Present language aim *inductively*: 
- provide text as demonstration of real / natural examples (news, websites, contextualized language)
- listening recorder dialogue / dictate
- read/listen first and then explain grammar vocab by themeselves 

Present languge aim *deductively*: 
- focus on form meaning 
- provide grammar rules / explanations 
- then use rules in interactions / exercises 

Anyway - examples should be meaningful and contextualized 

Don't just explain rules - include in context 

End presentation phase - refocus attention on lesson's aims, explain tasks ahead to practice language 


---

>**PRACTICE STAGE**

Practice / apply new language - controlled / monitored activities: 
- close-extended
- specific target objectives
- one/few correct answers 

Multiple activities in plan: 
- practice different skills areas 
- use different groupings 
- progress from easy-to-hard
- progress from close-ended comprehension-based (read/listen) to open-ended creation-based (write/speak)

Example practice activities:
- true/false
- fill-in-blanks
- sentence completion
- short answers 

---

>**REVIEW & HOMEWORK STAGES**

*Review stage*:
- review learning aims / concepts / material 
- check for comprehension
- clarify questions if any 
- feedback on strengths / weaknesses in studs performance 

*Homework stage*: 
- clarifying oral/written format tasks to complete outside class 
- Need time to explain directions and allow for questions 
- Best way - start assignment together and then let them finish 
- Type / frequency of homework depends on class / school / tutoring 

Homework: very important component to create successful multilingual learners - chance for students to decipher/internalize materials at own pace and with own learning practices

>**LESSON PLANNING FOR YOUNG LEARNERS**

Teaching to *Young Learners* (2-11 y.o.) has also unique characteristics - several considerations to be done 

-> *Cognitive development*:
- Young learners have different attention spans - be lively and engaging 
- Consider sequence of activities 
- Young learners stay on task better by setting up familiar routines and rotating activity types (calm/wake-up)
- Routine: always start with song and end with board race 

-> *Background and Life experiences*:
- Young learners have less life experience / cognitive capabilities 
- focus on concrete ideas and vocab they easy see/understand 
- As they grow  - proficiency / vocab more complex and abstract 


| AGE | COMMON TOPIC | COMMON ACTIVITY | 
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2-4 | animals, toys, body parts, colors, shapes, verbs, movements, numbers to 10 |  simple/repetitive songs, nursery rhymes, puppet shows, circle time, books read by teacher, games requireing movement| 
| 5-7 | animals, toys, food, drinks, body parts, shapes, colors, numbers, letters, holiday vocab, family members, clothes, jobs, daily activities | simple/repetitive songs, rhymes, puppet shows, drawing, describing, matching pictures, acti dialogue, make books, collect things, sort categories, cooperative games |
| 8-11 | animals, behavior, food, drinks, shapes, body parts, colors, letters, spelling, numbers, counting, telling time, maps directions, weather, seasons, routine, hobbies, clothes, family, jobs, feelings, verbs | simple songs, short readings, games with movement, drawing, describing, matching pictures, putting pictures in correct order, act dialogues or stories, complete speech from comics, map reading, giving directions  |

*Out of class workload*: 
- Homework might be not appropriate for all young learners
- However, schools may require assignments
- Others may need you to do your own 

**Language Production**:
- Below 5 years old - level of production very limited / non-existent 
- consider creating many short practice activities with no production 

**Highly Engaging Activities**:
- TPR activities - good for young learners 
- students respond to input by doing physical actions

>**ASSESSMENT**

Anyway part of lesson planning 

Teacher - constantly evaluate students/our objectives 

Differentiate: assessment & testing 

*Assessment* -> collect info on students language ability / achievement

*Testing* -> subset of assessment; seek formal measurement


**Summative VS Formative Assessment**:

- Summative assessment: 
    - allow determine level acquired up to point in time 
    - formal tests mid-term/final 

- Formative assessment: 
    - take place during instructional period
    - way to make adjustments to methods/materials/lesson-plan 
    - informal 
    - done at end of stage/lesson
    - checking comprehension in review stage 
        - assessment - measure extent of learning 
        - formative - used to inform creatin of objectives / lessons 

Teacher create unique assessment - example: pair role-plays

Assessment should be done orally one a comparable topic to mirror the role-play 

Match assessments and classroom practices - better chance success 


**Test - Teach - Test Lesson Format (TTT)**:

Alternative to PPP 

Formative assessment as substantial stages of lesson 

1. *Test*
    - assessment target structure / skill 
    - students use already have knowledge

2. *Teach*
    - use results assessment to structure plan lesson 
    - highlight previous knowledge 
    - students exercise practice 
    - teacher facilitate 

3. *Test*
    - monitor sstudents language usage 
    - use delayed error connection to focus on improvements

>**LESSON PLAN DELIVERY TIPS**

1. *Open class in consistent manner*
- open always same way 
- arrive and set up 10 minutes before 
- consider display schedule / plan on board 
- begin with greeting 

2. *Scaffold your activities* 
- sceffold activities by tapping into students background knowledge
- review point from previous lesson with warm-up 
- make students aware of goal 
- provide clear instructions for all activities 
- make sure they understand what they are expected to do 

3. *Provide time and space*
- give time limit for each activity 
- once working on practice / production stages - give them space / take yourself out of pictures
- make time announcement before end 

4. Have clear transactions 
- when time up, get attention and begin debrief / transition 
- use new knowledge to scaffold next activity 
- always have filler activities at hand
