**Assessment of Vocabulary**

To ensure validity, any formal or informal assessment of vocabulary must reflect activities that have been practiced in the classroom. For example, it is not fair to test students with cloze examinations if they have never done a similar cloze exercise in class before. With this in mind, vocabulary can be assessed by giving the target vocabulary word in a sentence and giving multiple choice answers from which the student must choose the correct synonym, antonym, or definition. Vocabulary can also be assessed in the context of speaking and short-answer or essay-writing activities.

Within the context of a speaking task, vocabulary use may constitute a part of each student’s overall oral skills assessment, as defined by a carefully prepared rubric. The context of the speaking task should match the vocabulary that is being tested, and the students should be made aware that they are expected to actively use the target **lexis**. For a short-answer assessment, you can provide open questions that include the target vocabulary, telling students that you expect their answers to reflect that they understand the question (including the target word or words).

The use of visuals is another possible **assessment technique** for vocabulary, where students must match vocabulary items to images. For example, if you are targeting students’ understanding of various rooms and features of a house, you can supply an unlabeled cutaway image of a house and have the students label the target room/items (while clearly signaling what/where you want the students to label).

Another way to assess specific vocabulary can involve error analysis or correction. This type of assessment includes Grammaticality Judgement Tests (in which a list of sentences is provided, some of which include lexical errors, and the students must identify which have errors and which are correct) and **Error Correction** (in which a list of sentences or a passage contains several errors to be corrected by the students). An example of a lexical error might include the wrong form of a word (e.g., “academy” where the adjective “academic” should be) or the placement of an obviously incorrect word (e.g., “I park my car in the library” - the student should mark this incorrect and/or replace “library” with “garage”).
