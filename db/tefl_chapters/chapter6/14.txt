**Vocabulary Teaching and Learning**

It might seem obvious that you will include vocabulary instruction in your language classroom, but before we look at some ways that you do that, we should first consider the importance of helping students develop their own vocabulary-building strategies. After all, students can only be exposed to and learn a limited amount of vocabulary within their language classes, so their proficiency will grow much faster if we equip them to actively acquire vocabulary outside of the classroom as well. This notion is backed up by Freeman and Freeman (2004), who state that, “**extensive reading** is the best way to build a rich vocabulary” (p. 204). As you will see in the chapter on reading, _extensive reading_ is self-selected pleasure reading done outside of the classroom (whereas the close reading for language learning that we do in the classroom is **intensive reading**).

Thus, encouraging wide reading in English is important, but we as teachers can also teach our students how to learn vocabulary independently. This can include:

- Vocabulary-in-context activities (**practice** with guessing meaning based on surrounding text and clues)
- Use of student-created mnemonic (memory) devices
- Self-selected entries in vocabulary notebooks or index cards
- Effective use of a dictionary when encountering new words in context
- Using knowledge of word parts, such as prefixes and suffixes, to help decipher meanings of new words

Alongside the teaching of these strategies and the encouragement of extensive reading, **ESL/EFL** teachers can and should integrate explicit vocabulary instruction. When doing so, there are several variables to be considered and decisions you need to make. The first of these is selection, meaning how to decide which words you will teach within your lesson plans, which should be directly related to the level of the learner and the skill and communicative goals of the lesson. This leads us to the second decision, which is related to _how you will teach and focus on the target words._ Of course, both of these decisions will affect your lesson planning, and there are various ways to implement a focus on vocabulary within the stages of our **Presentation-Practice-Production** format.
