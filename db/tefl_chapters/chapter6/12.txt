Analysis of Sample Vocabulary Activity  
Teachers need to be aware of how their classroom activities prepare their learners to understand and use vocabulary words. To that end, let’s focus on a sample vocabulary activity to determine which of Freeman and Freeman’s areas of linguistic understanding are being covered.

Vocabulary Exercise  

A class that consists of 14 beginner-level students has read a short text that contains the following seven adjectives:  

new cheap pretty easy hot right fast  

Part A: First, 14 cards are handed out, 7 of which contain the adjectives from the text, and 7 of which contain the antonyms of those adjectives. The students are told to get up and find the student who has the antonym for their word.  

| new | old | easy | hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| cheap | expensive | hot | cold |
| pretty | ugly | right | wrong |
|    |    | fast | slow |

Part B: Next, each pair must write a simple sentence using both of their words in connection with example objects. The teacher models this by supplying an example sentence using different adjectives:  

big/small  

A mouse is small, but an elephant is big.  

Once each pair has created a sentence like the example, they will write that sentence on the board using blank spaces instead of the adjectives. The teacher models with the example sentence:  

A mouse is ______, but an elephant is ______.  

Part C: Now that there are seven sentences on the board, students (working on paper and in the same pairs as before) work to fill in the blanks with the appropriate adjectives/antonyms.
