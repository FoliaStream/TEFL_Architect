**Selection**

The vocabulary you teach in your classroom will depend significantly on the students' level, their age, and their purpose for learning English (academic vs. basic conversational/social interaction skills). One good vocabulary resource for beginning **ESL/EFL** instructors is *The ESL Teacher's Book of Lists* by Jacqueline E. Kress, Ed.D. (1993). There are also several websites that feature word lists, some of which are listed in Appendix A of this chapter.

For beginning learners, it makes sense to promote **acquisition** of the most commonly used words. Common verbs include *be, go, like, look, listen, feel, see, hear, know, understand, get,* and so on. Nouns for beginners include basic emotions, parts of the body, family-related words, common professions, names of common foods, and everyday objects (*tree, house, room, school, etc.*) and tools (*computer, car, phone, pen, etc.*). Fortunately, linguists today have a much better idea of what constitutes the most frequently used words in spoken and written communication, and there are many compiled *wordlists* available on the internet that provide that information. When choosing vocabulary for young learners, it is recommended to begin with concrete nouns (objects we can see, touch, smell, taste, or hear). Around the ages of 10-12, young learners are better able to learn vocabulary related to abstract concepts such as qualities like *freedom* and concepts like *success* (Divine, 2019).

Given that we also want to **practice language skills** (reading/listening/speaking/writing) in our classrooms, another **method** of selecting words for explicit teaching is to select words from within our input texts (readings and listenings) that we think might be new for the students and/or useful in aiding in comprehension of those texts. As you gain teaching experience, you will become quite adept at selecting words you anticipate students will not understand. It is also recommended that students are given multiple encounters with new words along with opportunities to use them. Thematic teaching is one of the best ways to accomplish this.
