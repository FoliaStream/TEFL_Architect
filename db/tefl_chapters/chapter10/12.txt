**Table 1** outlines more suggestions on how to sequence and use videos in your classes.

**Table 1. Suggestions for integrating videos into language lessons**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-viewing activities (in groups or as a class) | ● Activate prior knowledge: design discussion questions to find out what students already know about this topic. ● Review the title or summary of video and guess the full content or main message of video. ● Review unfamiliar slang and other vocabulary students will encounter. ● Watch a short section of the video on silent and have students predict the topic and language they will hear. ● Snip pictures from the video or find pictures that relate to the audio and have students predict the topic and language they will hear. |
| While-viewing activities    | ● Take notes or answer general comprehension questions as a segment is playing (e.g., answer multiple choice questions or true-false questions; complete graphic organizers). ● Play a segment once without video so that students can guess the setting and predict what they will see. ● Break the video or audio into sections and have students guess what came before or after certain parts. |
| After-viewing activities (in groups or as a class) | ● Compare answers to comprehension questions. ● Review challenges in comprehension. ● Replay segment or key scenes as necessary. ● Analyze why the character(s) acted as they did. ● Predict what will happen in the next segment. ● Act out a scene with partner(s) as you remember it without a script - use your own words. ● Write a letter or letters from the point of view of one of the characters. |
