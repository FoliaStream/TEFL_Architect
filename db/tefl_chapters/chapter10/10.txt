**Wikis**

As noted earlier, wikis are generally more formal than blogs. They are useful for creating group essays or other collaborative projects. For instance, let’s say you assign group research projects to an advanced-level **ESL/EEL** class, where each group must research and report on a country no one in the class has visited. Specifically, imagine that each student in a group of four must report on one of the following: demographics (nationality, religion, language, population, etc.); political structure; important cultural traditions; or important historical landmarks. Students can create a wiki and type up each of their sections separately or in pairs, but they should be responsible for proofreading another group member’s section. They might also add content where appropriate. From this group essay, they can make a poster to present to the class, illustrating essential points. Some researchers argue that wikis allow students to combine their linguistic skills and create more grammatically accurate texts (Storch, 2011). Still, as with any project, the teacher must give clear directions on how to collaborate on the wiki, and make sure that every student understands the expectations of the assignment.
