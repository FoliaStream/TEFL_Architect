**Technology: The Rise in Online Instruction**

We are surrounded by technology that is rapidly evolving, with many of our students having grown up using the internet and smartphones. Not surprisingly, the pervasiveness of technology has impacted the **TEFL** profession. There are increasing numbers of language schools and students who use online tools and platforms to supplement or replace face-to-face language instruction. As such, it important for you to first reflect upon the differences in these types of instruction.

**Blended learning** is an approach that combines both traditional classroom teaching and online educational tools to create a hybrid or “blended” learning experience. In blended classes, students have face-to-face classes with an instructor as well as components of computer-mediated activities such as recordings, **wilds**, or quizzes that teach or assess content.

**Synchronous classes** are classes that take place entirely online and have a component that requires the student and teacher to both be online at a specified time. Synchronous courses can include phone conferencing, video conferencing, or chat. There are many online language schools that offer synchronous **EFL** classes.

**Asynchronous classes**, in contrast to blended and synchronous classes, are online lessons that do not require students to be online at any particular time. These classes are often pre-developed courses consisting of content or modules that students work through without the real-time assistance of an instructor. Asynchronous classes can operate through LMS (Learning Management System) platforms, e-mail, or other collaborative online means. Though it is certainly possible, it is not common for **EFL** teachers to teach asynchronous language classes, as most schools are seeking native speakers to interact directly with their students either face-to-face, online, or a combination of the two (Angelina et al., 2019).

**Pros and cons of online teaching and learning**

Not surprisingly, there are both pros and cons to learning and teaching in an online environment. The following offers a non-exhaustive list that addresses this topic:

- **Pros of online learning/teaching**

  ✔ Offers the convenience of location and time (e.g., no commute)

  ✔ Allows for learning in distant or disadvantaged locations
  
  ✔ More class/tutoring options available (e.g., type of instructors, courses, prices)

- **Cons of online learning/teaching**
  
  ✔ A face-to-face classroom environment may be the most familiar and comfortable learning for some students
  
  ✔ Building teacher **rapport** can be more challenging or take more time in a virtual setting
  
  ✔ Tech difficulties and lag time can negatively affect communication
  
  ✔ More challenging to attain the social benefits that come with face-to-face peer interaction. (e.g., forming friendships or professional connections).
