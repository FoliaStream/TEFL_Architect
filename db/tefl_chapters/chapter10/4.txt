**Choosing Visual Aids**

Not all visual aids are appropriate for the **ESL/EFL** classroom. For example, imagine having Thai students who have lived their whole life in a tropical climate. It would be unfair (and perhaps not relevant) to ask them to fully describe pictures of a ski resort in the mountains of Switzerland without reviewing their background knowledge and assisting with vocabulary and geography. Can you think of other characteristics of a visual aid that would detract from learning instead of enhancing it? Have you ever been exposed to poor visual aids as a student?

**Figure 1** lists some additional visuals to be avoided in ESL/EFL classrooms. How does this list compare to your own?

- ✓ Violent scenes
- ✓ Images that are too small or not clearly defined
- ✓ Poor reproductions

- ✓ Crowded or cluttered images
- ✓ Stereotyped images
- ✓ Poorly scaled illustrations
- ✓ Images unrelated to the lesson

In addition to the above guidelines, be aware of any culture-specific taboos your students may hold. For example, in some countries it may not be appropriate to photograph women. There may also be strict rules governing their dress. To give an example related to this, a major controversy arose after Ikea, a Swedish furniture company, found that women had been photostropped out of catalogs distributed in Saudi Arabia.
