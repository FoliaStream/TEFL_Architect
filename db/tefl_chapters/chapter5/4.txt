**Basic Steps in Course Design**

Teaching the same students over an extended period requires thoughtful big-picture planning. As a language teacher, you must decide *how* you want to teach, which is related to **method/approach** and the planning of individual lessons and activities, but you also have to know what to teach. As you will see in future chapters, this question involves grammar, **texts**, and **practice** of the four **language skills**, but it also involves looking at the bigger picture and considering how these things fit together over time.

*Overview of steps*

There are several facets involved in designing and delivering a course of study. You may be employed by a school that completes the process ahead of time, or you may be asked to complete some of the process as part of your employment. In any case, you should be aware of the series of steps that go into the design of a quality course of instruction. **Figure 1** provides a general list of those steps.

---

*Figure 1*. Steps in course design

| Step 1 | Identify situation & students, accounting for needs & priorities of school/program |
|---|---|
| Step 2 | Deliver **needs analysis** of students |
| Step 3 | Translate data from needs analysis into **course goals** and learning objectives |
| Step 4 | Determine course content, sequence, and structure. Evaluate and/or choose course materials, including textbooks |
| Step 5 | Design course syllabus, including schedule of units/assignments/assessments Evaluate and/or choose course materials, including textbooks |
| Step 6 | Construct lesson plans |
| Step 7 | Teach! Revise steps 4-6 as needed |
| Step 8 | Assess Use data to revise steps 4-6 |

---

*Adapted from Brown & Lee (2015)*

Again, you may not need to complete each of these steps, depending on the school and situation, but you need to be aware of the process and stand ready to complete some or all of the steps. You may have noticed that the evaluation and choosing of materials is included in both the 4th and 5th steps. You could find yourself selecting and using a textbook, for example, as part of your sequencing of the course in the 4th step. Alternatively, materials and textbooks can be selected in the 5th step, as part of syllabus construction.
