**Syllabus**

A textbook is only one example of the materials you may need to prepare prior to the first day of class. **Course documents** such as a syllabus can be a useful tool of communication when you meet your students for the first time, as it helps to establish broad timeframes for the **course goals** of study as well as some general classroom expectations.

As noted in previous chapters, the first few days are a critical period of teaching your new class, and thus the ideal time to make sure your syllabus is distributed and understood. This is the time where you will set the tone, pace, and foundation for the rest of your term. It's also a time to let your students know what you expect of them and what they can expect from you. A clear syllabus can help you achieve these important goals and create a positive and effective learning environment. Here is a non-exhaustive list of items commonly found in a syllabus:

- A bulleted list of easy to understand course goals
- A broad schedule of units of study, topics, and assessments in the course
- Information on grades and scores (e.g., numerical, letter)
- Your work (not personal) contact information
- Policies regarding tardiness
- Policies regarding late work
- Any additional policies such as cell phone use, target language use, academic honesty, classroom behavior, etc.

While much of the information on your syllabus may be decided by your program and institutional environment, it will be your responsibility to ensure that classroom expectations are fairly and consistently applied. Take time to review the syllabus with your students and answer any questions they may have. You may also want to give them a pop quiz on this information sometime during the first week to ensure **acquisition** (the quiz can be "open book").

Here are some sample quiz questions you can modify for your own syllabus quizzes:

1. What are we going to learn in this class?
2. What is the teacher's name and where is her/his office?
3. What days of the week and times do you have this class?
4. What is the name of the book we will use in this course?
5. Where can you find the due dates for your homework?
6. How many XX assignments do we have in this class?
7. How many points are the XX's assignments worth?
8. How many points do you need to pass this course?
9. What is the late work policy?
10. What happens if I miss a class?
11. What happens if I arrive late to class?
12. When is the last day of class?
