**Diversity in Language Courses**

Before we look at the steps and content needed to build a course of study, let us first step back to consider the diversity of language programs available today. Not surprisingly, language programs come in a variety of shapes and sizes, much of which stems from diverse attitudes toward what is viewed in various countries and/or schools as the most effective **method** to learn language. To best prepare you to enter the classroom, you should ask your school's administration some program-related questions prior to your first day, perhaps while interviewing.

Here are a few sample questions to get you started:

1. What are the larger learning goals of the language program? (e.g., test preparation, general English, full immersion, listening and speaking-focused, business English, etc.)
2. What are the learning methods and general teaching philosophies used by the school?
3. What materials, if any, are provided by the school? (books, lesson plans, syllabi, etc.)
4. How many hours a week does the average student study English in that program? (e.g., is the school an intensive language institute? Is homework expected?)
5. What days of the week and times of day do the classes take place?
6. Where are the classes to take place? (e.g., in public/private schools, the language institute, at the students' businesses, etc.)
7. How many instructors work at that school and in what capacity? (e.g., Will you co-teach? Does the school have a mentor program?)
8. What are the student demographics and how does that affect the program curriculum? (e.g., ages, backgrounds, motivations, etc.)
9. Is there a policy handbook regarding student behavior, attendance, etc?

While all programs employ diverse methods and structure (e.g., use of technology, the pace of the class, materials, etc.), they all have or require the same basic components needed in building a language course – learning objectives, lesson plans, materials, course policies, and some form of assessment.

Now that you have reflected on the diversity in programs, let's move on to addressing the basic steps taken in course design.
