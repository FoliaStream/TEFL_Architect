**Class Contract**

Creating a class contract is one technique to negotiate (to a certain extent) with your students on the rules for the classroom and the consequences for violating them. If the students are of the appropriate age and proficiency level, creating a class contract with them gives a sense of ownership in the classroom and helps reinforce good behavior.

Class contracts come in various forms. They can take the shape of more formal individual documents that students sign or they may take the form of colorful posters that you can hang on the wall of your classroom.

Figure 6 and Figure 7 demonstrate the diversity in class contracts. Note that the example in Figure 7 is blank, leaving room for groups of a more advanced proficiency level to create and negotiate the expectations with the teacher before signing.

Figure 6. Sample poster class contract

English Class Rules
1. Try to owl speak: English!
2. Come to class on time.
3. Raise your hand for questions.
4. Keep your eyes and ears on the person speaking.
5. Be hard to each other!
6. Try every activity.
7. Make mistakes. That's how we learn!

Signed by all of us below!

Regardless of contract appearance or age of your learners, take time to discuss the contents and make sure everyone understands the expectations. You should also periodically review and remind them of your policies throughout the term. Whatever you decide, enact your class policies and procedures early on, and apply them from an approach of positive reinforcement.

In either the syllabus or the class contract, you might also start training students in proper class etiquette and language, such as raising one's hand to ask and answer questions. Depending on the level of the students, you might even include questions to ask in common situations:

How do I pronounce ______?

How do you spell ______?

What does ______ mean?

I don't understand ______. Could you explain it?
