**Authentic and Realistic Listening Materials**

With the advent of modern technology and communication, there is no shortage of listening material available to teachers. In fact, there is so much material available that the question becomes one of narrowing down what exactly to use in the classroom. While there is no one criterion that fits all circumstances, the following guidelines will serve to make your listening lessons more positive by helping you choose good materials.

As you learned in chapter 2, there is a distinction to be made between realistic vs. authentic materials. **Realistic materials** are those that have been developed with the multilingual language learner specifically in mind; they generally accompany **ESL/EFL** texts or are produced by the teacher for specific students. *Authentic materials* are real-world selections produced for a native English-speaking audience, without consideration for multilingual learners.

Realistic materials range in quality and can be quite good, but as a rule, it is preferable to use authentic listening selections in class for the following reasons:

- Authentic materials reflect what learners are likely to encounter in real life, providing them with and preparing them for real-world experiences.
- They expose the listener to natural speech and varieties of English accents, vocabulary, and pronunciation features, and illustrate the contrast between the written and spoken word.
- They provide familiarity with common features of **discourse**, such as filler and hesitation sounds, conversation overlaps, false starts, incomplete utterances, etc., which helps build a tolerance for ambiguity in the second language learner.
- The listener can become more aware of the role of **stress**, **rhythm**, and **intonation** in conveying meaning.
- They increase the learner’s mental interaction with and processing of language input, thus increasing overall language acquisition.

Of course, much of what you choose will depend on your school’s teaching environment, course requirements, and the resources available to you. Regardless, strive to use authentic listening materials when and where possible – using them in support of any assigned realistic materials is also an effective teaching strategy.

Because authentic materials may be challenging for ESL students, it is important to consider the length of the audio and/or video you select. For in-class practices, listenings range in length from a few seconds to a few minutes, depending on the listening focus. Rarely do authentic in-class listening tasks extend past four minutes. If the materials you want to use extend past this suggested time, consider breaking up the content and using a shorter segment in class.

The age of your students is another important consideration when choosing authentic (and realistic) listening materials. For younger learners, it is generally best to use shorter clips with simple topics, and the ideas within should be more concrete than abstract in nature.

Finally, you should consider your students’ individual wants and needs when selecting topics. For instance, adult students may have a need or want for specific listening tasks related to business or academic English.
