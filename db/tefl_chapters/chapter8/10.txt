**Final Thoughts on Teaching Listening**

In order to truly meet the needs of your students, it is important that you formulate a total listening approach - strategies, techniques, and activities – at the onset of any course and in all your lessons. This should be done for even the lowest-level student since listening is the primary skill for acquiring new language. Remember that it is an active skill rather than a passive one, so any listening activities you do must take advantage of and build upon the learners’ existing knowledge and mental processing capabilities; pre-teaching works very well in this regard.

Use a combination of top-down, bottom-up, and interactive activities from both an intensive and extensive standpoint. Within that approach, incorporate a wide variety of meaningful, comprehensible, and authentic materials that have been adapted to the age, level, interests, needs, and goals of your students, all within a comfortable learning environment. Finally, always keep in mind that to ensure true learning of new language concepts, you should follow listening activities with a production activity based on what students heard earlier in the lesson.

By adhering to the principles outlined above, you will ensure that your listening tasks will be as effective and efficient as possible and that your students receive the maximum benefit from your teaching.
