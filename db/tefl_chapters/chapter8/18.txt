**Lesson Planning – Setting up an Intensive Reading**

Similar to setting up a listening, a good reading lesson requires proper preparation on the part of the instructor. Before introducing a reading into your class, consider the following tips:

- Make sure that you have sufficient copies for your students.
- Preface the reading with pre-reading tasks that trigger background knowledge and assist with new language features.
- Prepare tasks that address both **bottom-up** and **top-down** reading skills. For example, you might prepare main idea or detail questions about the main characters, setting, important dates, or sequence of events. Main idea questions demonstrate key points in the text, while detail questions support or show examples, comparisons, reasons, or facts that connect to the larger main ideas.
- Prepare a few inference questions that require learners to read between the lines. For example, you might ask students to guess where the story takes place, or you might ask learners to guess the relationship between the characters. Inference questions should not be explicitly stated in the reading and should be derived.
- Number the paragraph be add word count numbers to the text so that students can more easily reference the text.
- Use the reading as a foundation for a **production task**.
- Prepare supplementary/extension tasks.
