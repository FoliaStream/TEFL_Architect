**Lesson Planning – Setting up a Listening**

A good listening lesson, like any other lesson, requires proper preparation on the part of the instructor. Aside from your lesson plan and choice of materials, it is important to ensure these conditions are met:

- Make sure the technology works! There is always the potential for unexpected problems - DVDs get scratched and skip or won’t play, weblinks expire, volume/dairly is poor, etc. Test the playback equipment and media to confirm that everything is functioning properly and that sound/video quality is acceptable.
- Prepare supplementary materials in case of unexpected tech problems. For instance, you can read the dialogue if the audio will not play.
- Prepare a pre-listening activity to activate your students' **schema** and background knowledge. This will prepare them mentally to accept new language. (The next section will give ideas on how to do this.)
- Prepare both extensive and intensive tasks to ensure that you give your students adequate **practice** on both.
- Print the audio transcript if your post-comprehension tasks call for reading the audio. However, do not let your students _read_ to practice listening!
- Ensure that your listening **task** aligns with your lesson goals. Listening needs to be more than isolated exercises with no connection to the syllabus or goal(s) of the course.
- Use the listening lesson as the foundation for a **production** activity (speaking and/or writing); to internalize language, output should follow input.
