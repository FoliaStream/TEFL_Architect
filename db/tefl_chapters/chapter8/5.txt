**Types of Listening – Intensive and Extensive**

This section will review the two core types of listening that parallel the brain’s listening process and will also provide example practice activities.

>*Intensive listening* involves listening with a specific purpose in mind (i.e., listening for details and selective information). It is akin to bottom-up processing because it focuses on more specific features of the aural input. Activities to practice this can take many forms, including the following tasks:

- Prediction questions to focus attention on specific information
- True/false and yes/no questions for specific details
- Why-questions for specific information (open-ended or multiple choice)
- Close and fill-in-the-blank exercises
- Sentence completion
- Error correction
- Listening for sound and pronunciation features
- Ticking, numbering, or reordering pictures
- Filling out graphic organizers, forms, charts, and tables

>*Extensive listening* means listening for holistic ideas such as gist, context, and speakers’ functions, purposes, and attitudes. It is akin to top-down processing because it is more holistic in nature. Types of exercises to practice this may include the following tasks (note that summarizing, paraphrasing, and synthesizing need to be pre-taught):

- Prediction questions to focus attention on concepts such as main idea or setting
- Main idea questions (e.g., true/false, yes/no, why-questions)
- Differential questions (e.g., true/false, yes/no, why-questions)
- Critical responses that ask learners to make a connection between their background knowledge and the listening
- Critical responses that ask learners to give an opinion or extrapolate information from the listening into a new context
- Summary or paraphrase that requires information from the listening
- Synthesis of multiple listenings
