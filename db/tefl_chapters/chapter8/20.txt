** In-Class Reading Activities

- **Skimming**

Skimming is the act of looking over a text quickly for the general topic or theme. One way to practice skimming in class is to set a time limit on reading a text and provide follow-up questions about the main idea(s). To assist in skimming, teach students to start by reading titles, headings, and topic sentences, and to skip over unimportant words. If students are skimming longer texts, teach them to also read the first and last (introductory and conclusion) paragraphs.

---

- **Scanning**: 

Is the act of looking over text quickly for specific information. One way to practice scanning is to give your students closed, detail-based questions on a text such as a travel brochure and then have them quickly "scan" the brochure to answer the questions. To assist students in scanning, teach them to skip over large text areas without trying to read or understand them. If possible, tell learners to keep the image of the target information (which is often a date, name, or single word) in their mind as they "scan" through the text. If students are scanning longer texts, teach them to first look to the table of contents, index, or glossary, whenever applicable.

---

- **Skimming**

Speed reading is closely linked to the concept of skimming and scanning. As in the above activities, students are encouraged to read “quickly.” There are many types of speed-reading activities. One example would be to monitor reading by giving students a brief passage and having them write down the time it took to finish it, or you can take the reading away after a set amount of time and give them simple questions to assess understanding. If they read quickly but cannot accurately answer the questions, they are not reading effectively. If they are taking a long time to read but answer the questions correctly, they will need strategies to read faster. In either case, the teacher might go through the reading section by section and point out the phrases they should skim over, as well as other strategies, such as marking the text, that can improve reading speed without compromising comprehension.

---

- **Running dictation** 

is a fun activity that takes the form of a game and is used primarily with younger learners at the beginner or low-intermediate levels. Below are steps for executing this dictation activity in class (adapted from Nation and Newton, 2009, p.62):

- Place a short reading on the back wall or on the outside of the classroom door.
- Have students get into pairs and decide who will be Student A and Student B.
- Student A begins by running to the passage, reading the text, and returning to dictate what they can remember to Student B.
- Student B writes the information on a piece of paper while Student A assists with correctness.
- The roles of A and B are switched and they continue to run and dictate until the entire passage has been transcribed correctly.

---

- **Stunning**

Reading aloud is another activity that you can use in your classes. Reading aloud can take various forms such as the teacher reading to the class while students listen and follow the text with their eyes, individual students reading to the class, or students reading in pairs or small groups. There are many benefits that come with these activities, such as increasing classroom unity, practicing student pronunciation (e.g., stress, intonation), and developing sound-spelling awareness. As with any practice task, keep this activity within a reasonable time limit.

---

- **Reading Jigsaws** 

Are cooperative multi-skill activities where students must become “experts” on a section of a reading in order to teach others the content from that section. After reading individually, students are placed into groups where they must speak, listen, and write in order to complete a given task.
