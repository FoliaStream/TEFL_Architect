**Listening Sequence**

- *Pre-Listening*
- *During-Listening*
- *Post-Listening*

---

**Pre-Listening** - The purpose of the pre-listening phase is to mentally and linguistically prepare students for the topic by triggering background knowledge, engaging interest, and pre-teaching key vocab and/or grammar. Possible actions to achieve this include the following tasks:

- Having paired discussion questions related to the topic
- Pre-teaching needed vocabulary
- Pre-teaching needed background information
- Using stimulating pictures or **realla** related to the topic
- Playing part of the video or audio and asking prediction or setting questions
- Playing video on silent or freeze-framing a scene and asking prediction questions


While widely used, this pre/ouring/post approach is not without its detractors, who generally feel that it focuses too much on the product as opposed to the process. That is to say, if over-emphasizes listening for information instead of teaching learners _how_ or listen. Critics of this comprehension approach believe that for listening instruction to be truly effective, it must involve the listener decoding and extracting the speaker’s message and its meaning. Instead of just answering discrete information questions. However, the comprehension approach can be adjusted to accommodate these concerns by following these guidelines:

1. Build tolerance for ambiguity. Use open-ended as opposed to close-ended questions and ask students to discuss possible answers and give justification for them. Remind your students that not understanding _everything_ does not equate to not understanding _anything_ - comprehension need not be at 100% in order to understand the overall message.
2. Have exercises that focus attention on sounds and **discourse** features that carry meaning. Examples of this include turn-taking signals and other **discourse markers**, **stress**, **rhythm**, and **intonation** patterns; linked sounds and boundaries between words and thought groups; or strategic pauses by the speaker.


--- 

**During-Listening** - The purpose of the during-listening phase is to gain practice in building intensive and/or **extensive listening** skills. A potential sequence follows these four steps:

- Play the audio to attune learners to the recording and gain practice in extensive listening skills.
- Unavoidably provide each student with tasks or questions to check comprehension.
- Listen a second time to allow students to individually confirm answers. Allow students to then select whether the listening needs further repetition.
- Discuss answers in pairs and then review as a class.

A common complement to this four-step sequence would be a review of sound features and/or new language items, as well as a potential final listening with students following and/or marking the transcript.


---

**Post-Listening** - The purpose of the post-listening phase is to gain greater exposure and practice with the target language by building from the listening into a related **production task** or tasks (speaking or writing). In other words, the post-listening **stage** generally contains further activities that feature the topic and language features of the listening, activities that eventually build from input toward output, culminating in a production activity that allows learners to apply what they have just heard. Possible actions to achieve this include the following tasks:

- Relating listening to your students’ lives in a speaking or writing task  
- Role playing conversations connected to the listening topic  
- Writing and/or saying what happened before/will happen next  
- Giving advice to speakers  
- Drawing scenes from the listening and presenting them
