**Experiential Learning**

In order for language to be remembered, it is not enough just to present the information to students. Lecturing will never bring much success in any language classroom. If we really want our students to learn English, we need to give them many opportunities to use the language in many different real-life situations. To do this, we must find ways to bring authentic situations to students whose exposure to English is limited to a few class hours per week.

Try to make your lessons as realistic as possible by bringing realia, props, and authentic materials to the classroom. Make use of role plays, simulations, debates, presentations, case studies, and drama. Try to involve “students in doing things and thinking about the things they are doing” (Bonwell & Eison, 1991, p. 2).

According to Meyers and Jones (1993, p. xi), teachers should create many “opportunities for students to talk and listen, read, write, and reflect as they approach course content through problem-solving exercises, informal small groups, situations, case studies, role plays, and other activities - all of which require students to apply what they have been learning.”

Reflect on your own high school and/or college learning experience. Did you benefit from lectures, assigned reading material, or class discussions? Did you have many opportunities to work in groups and teach your peers? What types of activities worked best for you and why? Which ones did not work for you? Keep these questions in mind as you read the following sections on creating classroom activities.
