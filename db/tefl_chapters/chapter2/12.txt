**Realia**

Realia are everyday objects that can be brought to class and used as props. The list of potential realia is infinite and leaves a lot of room for creativity. By manipulating these objects, students memorize vocabulary more easily and make associations not only with the written and spoken word but also with the physical item. Realia is useful in classrooms with students of any age but can be especially useful with young learners who are not yet able to read or write in their native language.

Realia can be used to practice both vocabulary and grammar. For example, ask your students to bring some additional items of clothing. In class, practice giving and following directions, such as, “put your coat on,” “wrap the scarf around your neck,” “take off your hat,” etc.

Another example is to ask students to collect leaves, sticks, and rocks. In class, students compare colors, sizes, and shapes. You can introduce and practice comparatives (e.g., “the green leaf is bigger than the yellow leaf”) and superlatives (e.g., “the red leaf is the biggest”).
