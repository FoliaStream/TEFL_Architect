**Autonomous Learning**

Another term associated with a student-centered classroom is autonomous learning. Autonomous learning is when students take responsibility for their own learning. As we just discussed in its one of the teacher’s roles to help students realize that they will only become successful when they are truly involved and actively participating in the learning process. The teacher needs to help students understand that they are studying for themselves and that all activities covered in class are for their benefit, even quizzes and exams.

For this reason, the teacher should be open to students’ suggestions on topics for class discussions, reading or writing assignments, and even the content of a test. Look at Figure 1 and consider the questions about the four elements of a lesson: content, process, product, and evaluation. In a student-centered classroom, any or all of these components can be decided by the teacher, by the students, or by both together (Pappas, 2011).

Regardless of who makes those choices, it is crucial to present the new material and activities in a meaningful and structured way. The teacher should be in control and monitor the students’ progress and needs at all times so that the lesson does not lose purpose or focus.

Encouraging autonomous learning is a gradual process that needs to start with students setting goals for themselves (e.g. “this month I want to focus on improving my reading skills”) and reflecting on their own learning progress (e.g. “during this week I have learned how to use Present Perfect Tense”). This will enhance students’ intrinsic motivation. According to Scharle and Szabo (2000), intrinsically motivated students are more willing to take responsibility for the outcome (i.e. ?).
