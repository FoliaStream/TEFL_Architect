**Varying Activities**

*Include multiple language skills*

You should strive to incorporate all four language skills into your classes. For example, always include a reading text for project work in the form of either a model reply or a text that they have to read and discuss in their groups. If you provide your students with an activity worksheet, it will also give them reading practice. While they are discussing the text in their groups, they practice listening and speaking skills, and when they complete the worksheet, they practice their writing skills. When they present their work to other classmates, they practice their presentation skills. Try to include some kind of a writing assignment for groups to complete; it helps them practice all four language skills.

Other sample activities that help students get used to different classroom dynamics include mingling activities, questionnaires, and interviews. Figure 8 illustrates a mingle activity for beginners. Students stand up and interview their classmates that do not sit next to them. Afterward, they describe one person they interviewed, and other students try to guess who it was.

*Use kinesthetic learning*

As mentioned in VARK Learning Styles Model, the term kinesthetic learning can be thought of as incorporating physical movement or touch to stimulate learning (Lengel & Kuczala, 2010), and kinesthetic activities are the most effective in language classrooms when movements are tied to the language goal being taught. In other words, just having the students stand up at their desks and recite vocabulary words would not likely be as effective as having the students inline or act out those vocabulary words for their classmates. When appropriate, get your students up and moving by incorporating kinesthetic activities into your lessons. (This is especially true if you are teaching young learners.) See the following list for other ideas on how to create kinesthetic activities:

- Movements tied to commands, i.e., giving directions to draw, cook, move, etc.
- Actions that accompany lyrics to a song
- Acting out dialogues and performing skits
- Manipulating objects (especially realia)
- Game-based physical activities such as Simon Says, charades, pantomime, etc.

*Allow for student-to-student teaching*

Another way to vary your activities is to give students opportunities to teach difficult parts of the material to other students. Encourage students to prepare presentations in which they explain difficult grammar points to others in small groups or in front of the class. Your role would then be to monitor and to check the accuracy of the presented information. Teaching others can be an effective way to learn the material.
