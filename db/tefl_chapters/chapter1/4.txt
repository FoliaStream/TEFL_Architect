**English as a Global Language**

Today, English is regarded as a global lingua franca, or a common language, for many purposes such as print, education, and business. It is the official language in 67 different countries and 27 different non-sovereign entities. It is also the official language of many organizations including the United Nations, European Union, OPEC, NATO, and others. English is also the medium for an estimated 80% of the information stored in the world’s computers, accounts for three-quarters of the world’s mail, and is used in half of the world’s technical and scientific periodicals (Brown, 2009).

When looking at language statistics, it is important to remember that there are varying ways to measure and calculate how many people “speak” a language. Linguists commonly calculate language in two divisions: native (i.e. first language) speakers and non-native (i.e. second language) speakers (“second language” is commonly used for any language that is not a user’s first). Currently, English is estimated at having between 350-400 million people who identify as native speakers (Simons & Fennig, 2017). That number puts English native language speakers below the number of Chinese native speakers and, depending on the statistics used, also below Hindi and/or Spanish native speakers. However, when you combine the number of both native and second language speakers, the number of English speakers eclipses the others, with an estimated 1,500-1,800 million speakers worldwide.

Given the status of English as a lingua franca and the high number of native and non-native speakers, it is no surprise that market research companies predict the number of people actively learning English as a second language to increase. One such prediction projects the size of the global market for English Language Learning to reach nearly 70 billion dollars by 2029 (Adroit Market Research, 2021).
