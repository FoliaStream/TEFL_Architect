**Defining the Profession**

When considering the profession of teaching, we can see that it is a complex concept. Table 1 lists concepts of what teaching is and is not. Teaching is a career; it is different from a job because you will not just be on the clock when you are at school. You are always a teacher no matter where you are. For example, if you are shopping, you might be seen by your students. If you go out at night, you might be seen either by your students or a family member of theirs. If you go on vacation within the vicinity of where you live – of course this applies everywhere, but is most applicable to your nearby area and places that your students and their families frequent – remember that you are a teacher 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and thus need to act in a way that maintains the respect of students and colleagues.

*Table 1*. Defining the teaching profession

| Teaching is not... | Teaching is... |
|---|---|
| a job.    | a career.    |
| about you.    | about the students.    |
| about being your students’ friends | about being your students’ age-appropriate role model.    |
| about being an expert.    | making your students experts.    |
| static.    | about ever-changing and becoming better at your profession the more you practice it.    |
| being an individual separated from the community. | being a pillar of the community or communities in which you teach.    |
| being popular.    | being respected for your integrity, the way you care for your students, and your desire to help them to succeed. |

Adapted from Lumpkin (2008)
