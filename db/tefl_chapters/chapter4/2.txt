**Introduction**

All effective teachers have some type of plan when they enter their classroom; it can be an extended, detailed, step-by-step procedure, or just a series of short bullet points for more experienced teachers. As a beginning teacher, a lesson plan is an essential tool in order to become a competent and engaging teacher, but it has many other functions as well. It serves as a record of what you have covered in class and whether an activity was effective, as well as a reminder of what you wish to accomplish next. It will also help you plan and prepare future assessments. Finally, it is also an indicator of your professionalism as an **EFL** teacher.

Once you have created your plan, don't feel it is "the law". Rather, all experienced teachers know that *flexibility* in delivery is key to addressing individual student needs and maintaining good class management. As Jensen (2001) states, "a good lesson plan guides but does not dictate what and how we teach."
