**Warm-Up Stage**

The **warm-up stage** includes greeting your students and starting the lesson. It serves as the students' entry point into the English language and should be designed to help them ease into the second language in a low-pressure way. This may take the form of recycling a key component of the previous lesson or activating background knowledge and **schema** for the current one.

One common warm-up activity is to present one or two open-ended questions related to the upcoming topic and give students 2-3 minutes to discuss their answers in pairs or small groups. You can add pictures or **realia** to accompany the questions and encourage communication. For example, if the theme for the day deals with travel, you can bring some pictures or souvenirs of your own travel experiences in order to raise the students' interest and get them thinking about travel experiences of their own. Alternatively, you could show a busy scene of an airport, bus station or hotel and ask students to describe the picture in pairs or small groups.

Regardless of the theme, the questions should be easily answerable, requiring content and language that the learners already have (you don't want to ask questions that they cannot answer or require language that they will be learning in later stages). Figure 2 provides some example questions.

*Figure 2.* Sample questions for a warm-up stage

| Sample Lesson Theme | Sample Warm-Up Questions |
|---|---|
| Ordering Food in a Restaurant (Beginner)    | 1. What restaurants do you like? 2. What jobs are in a restaurant? 3. What do they do in those jobs? |
| Identity Theft (Intermediate)    | 1. Do you keep personal information such as bills and banking statements on the Internet? Why or why not? 2. What would you do if you lost your student ID, passport, or other photo ID? |
| Making a Good Impression (Advanced)    | 1. Do you think making eye contact is important when meeting people for the first time? Why or why not? 2. What are some personality behaviors employers may look for when hiring new people? |

Other common types of warm-up activities include brainstorming tasks such as creating a mind map or listing. Regardless of what activity you use in your warm-up, the questions or content should be easy and contain language that the learners already have.
