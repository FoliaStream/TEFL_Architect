**Lesson Planning for Young Learners**

When planning activities and lessons for young learners, many of the principles and ideas given in the previous sections are applicable. However, teaching young learners also has unique characteristics. Note that we use the term young learner to refer to students between the ages of 2-11. When teaching students of this age group, there are various considerations to keep in mind, and this section will address some of them in turn.

**Cognitive Development**
Young learners tend to have different attention spans than adults. As such, your lessons should strive to be lively and engaging. To start, consider the sequence of your activities. Many teachers find that young learners stay on **task** better by setting up familiar routines (e.g., always start with a song and end with a board race) and rotating activity types (e.g., calming activities versus wake-up activities).

**Background and Life Experiences**
Young learners have less life experience and cognitive capacities than adult learners. For that reason, it is best to focus on concrete ideas and vocabulary that students can easily see or understand. As young learners progress in age and proficiency, vocabulary can take on more complex forms and the introduction of more abstract vocabulary will become possible.

Young learners will have varying levels of development and thus you need to consider which activities and skills are appropriate for them. See **Table 1** for a list of common topics and activities for young learners.

---

*Table 1.* Common topics and activities for young learners

| | Common Topics    | Common Activities    |
|---|---|---|
| Age 2-4 | animals, toys, body parts, basic foods, basic colors, basic shapes, numbers to 10, basic means of transportation, common verbs (drink, eat, sleep), and verbs of movement (sit down, stand up, jump, run, dance, stop) | simple and repetitive **songs**, nursery rhymes, puppet shows, circle time, books read aloud by the teacher that have simple repetitive vocabulary, and games that require movement, such as animal charades or Simon Says |
| Age 5-7 | animals, toys, food and drink, body parts, shapes, colors, numbers, letters, holiday vocabulary, weather, seasons, days of the week, clothes, family members, means of transportation, furniture, jobs, feelings, school, common verbs, verbs of movement, and daily activities | simple and repetitive songs, nursery rhymes, puppet shows, drawing and describing (e.g., animals or monsters), matching pictures, acting out dialogues and stories, making books, collecting things (rocks, leaves, etc.), sorting things into categories, books read out loud by the teacher that have simple and repetitive vocabulary, cooperative games |
| Age 8-11 | animals and their behavior, food and drink, shapes, body parts, colors, letters, spelling, numbers, counting, telling time, maps and directions, holiday vocabulary, weather, seasons, days of the week, months, daily routines, hobbies, clothes, means of transportation, family members, furniture, jobs, feelings, school, common verbs, and verbs of movement | simple songs, short reading passages on an interesting topic, games that require movement (e.g., charades or Simon Says), drawing and describing (e.g., animals or monsters), matching pictures, putting pictures in the correct order, acting out dialogues and stories, making books, completing empty speech bubbles in comic books, treasure hunts, map reading, and giving directions |

*Taken from Teaching English to Young Learners ITA coursebook (Divine, 2019)*
