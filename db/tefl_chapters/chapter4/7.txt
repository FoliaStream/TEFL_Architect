**Lesson Plan Objectives**

Experienced teachers also know that the most important component of your lesson plan is your list of *objectives* (sometimes called ***alms*** – there are a variety of different terms used for objectives, the use of which varies by institution). For the sake of simplicity, we will consider learning objectives, which describe the *results* of your lesson(s). These objectives use action verbs and should be written from the perspective of what the learner will be able to do at the end of the session, not what the instructor will teach. For that reason, it is common to start by using the SWBAT format.

By the end of the lesson, **students will be able to...**

- successfully schedule a dentist appointment over the telephone.
- ask and answer pertinent questions and negotiate date and time.
- demonstrate comprehension of 10 relevant doctor appointment vocabulary items.
- produce questions using *when*, *where*, *what time*, and *how long*
- produce appropriate, polite forms in greeting, making requests, and closing a phone conversation.

- **Alms & Objectives**
- **Other elements you may include in a lesson plan are timing, assumptions, interaction, materials, and homework.**

**Timing:** How long will each activity take?

**Assumptions:** What must students be able to do, at the onset of the lesson, in order to successfully complete the lesson? For instance, in the objective described previously, you must assume that they can tell time in English and use the simple future tense. If students can't do this, they may not be able to complete the lesson.

**Interaction:** Who is speaking to whom in each part of the lesson? Make sure that the lesson is designed so that the amount of Student Talking Time (**STT**) is greater than Teacher Talking Time (**TTT**). The students should be doing most of the language **production** in any successful language class, so building STT directly into your plan can be helpful.

**Materials:** What books, activities, websites, etc. will you use? How will the student(s) interact with them?

**Homework:** What must students do as a follow-up to this lesson and before the next lesson?
