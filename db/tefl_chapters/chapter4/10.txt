**Introduction Stage**

The *introduction stage* is a short sub-stage that is designed to bring the theme of the lesson and the content of the **presentation** into focus. If the **warm-up** is connected to the lesson's theme, an introduction may not be necessary. However, it is often helpful to introduce the theme, find out what the students already know, and get the students thinking about the topic. This can be done through the use of images or **realia**, questions, or general discussion. For example, if the presentation takes a text about travel, the teacher may want to show some pictures of their own travel experiences in order to raise the students' interest and get them thinking about travel experiences of their own. When using pictures and images, be aware of including diverse representations in what you present. The introduction can also be a time to pre-teach some vocabulary that will be necessary to comprehend the presentation.

You also have the option to show students the activities you have planned on the board. It is perfectly acceptable to make in-class modifications to your plan in order to adjust to the changing needs of your students. In fact, all seasoned teachers make minor to major in-class modifications to their plan to enhance learning. If done appropriately, your class will appreciate your efforts to recognize their individual needs and be better prepared to achieve the lesson learning **aims**.
