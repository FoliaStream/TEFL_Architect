**Practice Stage**

In the *practice stage*, students are given the opportunity to practice and apply the new language through a series of controlled and monitored activities. Most practice activities are close-ended. Meaning, they have a specific targeted **objective** and perhaps have only one or a limited number of correct answers.

Often, a lesson plan will include multiple practice activities such that the students can practice the new language within different skill areas (reading, writing, listening, and speaking) and groupings (individual, pairs, groups, etc.). In general, these activities should progress from easier to more difficult and from close-ended comprehension-based to open-ended creation-based (if the practice stage includes any open-ended activities). In other words, activities often progress from receptive reading and/or listening tasks into productive writing and/or speaking tasks.

There are countless activities that can be done in the practice stage, and some examples are things like matching and true/false tasks, cloze (fill-in-the-blank) tasks, sentence completion, and short answer questions. These activities can take many forms depending on the skill area being practiced, but whatever the **task**, make sure you provide feedback, guidance, and correction as they work. In this stage of the lesson, the emphasis is on "we (teacher and students) do."
