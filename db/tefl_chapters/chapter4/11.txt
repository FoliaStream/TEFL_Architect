**Presentation Stage**

The *presentation stage* is where the learners are exposed to meaningful examples of the language concept of the particular lesson. This can either be done inductively by starting with use of language before discussing form and meaning or deductively by focusing on form/meaning before providing examples of its meaningful use. (See chapter 7 for more information on *form, meaning, and use* in teaching English.)

To present the language **aims** inductively, you can provide some sort of text that demonstrates realistic and natural examples of the language in use, which could mean a reading, a menu, a website, or anything with contextualized target language on it. This could also consist of some sort of listening, like a recorded dialog or dictation. Students can first read or listen to this material and then be asked to explain language features such as grammatical rules or vocabulary meanings for themselves before the teacher addresses them. By contrast, to present deductively, you can first focus on the form and meaning of the target language by providing students with grammatical rules or direct explanations of language features. Then, you can address examples of authentic language use by interacting with that language in speech or written texts.

Regardless of whether you use inductive or deductive means in the presentation stage, the language examples should be meaningful and contextualized. In other words, don't just explain some grammar rules or give a list of vocabulary words. The idea is to present examples of that grammar and/or to include the new vocabulary words within a meaningful context. It is also helpful if you take the time to connect the new language to what the students already know and bring to the lesson.

To give an example of a presentation stage, imagine that your learning aims include clothing vocabulary and comparative adjectives. You can read a short dialogue or listen to an audio on shopping where clothing items are compared and contrasted in a store. You can address the key grammar or vocabulary features before or after the language is contextualized.

Finally, you can end the presentation stage by refocusing their attention on the lesson's language aims and explaining that the tasks ahead will help them **practice** this language. Think of the presentation stage as "I (the teacher) facilitate a clear focus of the target language in context."
